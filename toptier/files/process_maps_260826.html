How to read these maps

Two different notations are used, on purpose, because they answer two different questions. Nothing on either page requires prior training. This legend is the whole of it.

Why two notations rather than one

Map A is a cross-functional flowchart, more commonly called a swimlane map. Each lane is a source a lead could come from. Lanes exist to make ownership and handoffs visible, and the question Map A has to answer is exactly that: which channel produced this lead, and did anyone write it down.

Map B is a service blueprint. Its bands are organized by what the homeowner can see rather than by job title. That matters here, because one person does most of the selling, so a map divided by role would produce one crowded lane and several empty ones. Divided by visibility instead, the same process reveals its real shape: everything the customer sees is well practiced, and almost everything behind that line is undefined.

Two conventions worth knowing

Metrics belong under the step, not in a separate dashboard. This comes from value stream mapping, where every process box carries a data box beneath it. A measurement that lives away from the work it measures drifts away from it too.

Current state and proposed state are never blended. Also standard practice, and the reason this document draws them twice. A map that quietly mixes what happens with what should happen cannot be trusted for either purpose.

One deliberate borrowing, declared

Service blueprints do not normally carry decision diamonds. Map B has one anyway, at the insurance versus retail fork, and it is drawn oversized. That fork is the highest-value decision in this business, and preserving notation purity is not worth burying it. Everything else on Map B is standard blueprint notation.

A note on sequence. No calendar durations appear anywhere in this document. Where order matters it is stated as a dependency: what must be true before the next thing can work. Dependencies are defensible. Invented week counts are not.

The fix is already proven inside their own data

34 of 34 cancellations carry a real, differentiated reason, because the cancel dialog requires one before it will close. Every other pick-list in the system is a default nobody changed. The pattern is not carelessness, it is design: they fill fields the workflow forces and skip fields it does not. Make Lead Source behave like Cancel Reason and it will be filled at the same rate. That is the argument for every proposed step below, and it is their own behavior read back to them rather than an outside opinion.

Map A, proposed state

Same lanes, same shape, with the gaps filled. Nothing here is a new system and nothing here is a new hire.

Proposed Six changes, none of them software purchases.
P-A1  Proposed
Make Lead Source a required field with a short, real pick-list
Modeled on the cancel dialog that already works. Referral, Repeat customer, Google search, Google Business Profile, Storm canvass, Jobsite sign, Social, Partner company, Other. Short enough to pick honestly, specific enough to be useful.
Dependency: gates everything else
P-A2  Proposed
Require a "Referred By" name whenever Referral is chosen
"Referral" as one bucket says nothing. "Referral, Keith's dad" against "Referral, past customer" tells them which engine is slowing and which is compounding.
Depends on: P-A1
P-A3  Proposed
Require Date of Loss on the insurance path, in one field only
Retire the duplicate field. This turns weather from an anecdote into a measurable demand signal, in a business where weather is the demand.
Depends on: the fork being a decision
P-A4  Proposed
Write the qualifying criteria down, on one page
Built with the owners, not handed to them. Is there damage and a plausible date of loss, has a claim been filed or would one be filed, is the property inside the service area, is the scope a whole roof or a repair, who owns the property. The purpose is not to turn work away. It is to choose the path on purpose.
Owner: Guy and Keith together
P-A5  Proposed
Build the review loop and make it a step, not a hope
A completed job triggers a review request. It attaches to the milestone template they already own, at Project Close Out, a stage that exists and has never been completed. Ask the three questions customers actually ask, so the reviews answer the objections before they are raised.
Closes the loop into A1 and A2
P-A6  Proposed
Add a second intake path, so a call arriving mid-roof still becomes a lead
A lead-generation fix wearing a sales costume. A call nobody answered was never a lead. See Map B, fail point F1.
Dependency: before any paid traffic
The dependency, stated as a dependency

Nothing in the channel column can be measured until the source field is fixed. That field is the gate on the entire marketing measurement program. Fix the field, then measure, then spend. Buying traffic before the field works means paying for leads that arrive with no way to tell where they came from, which is precisely the position the business is in today.

Phase 4, drawn at its real size

Everything above is drawn to scale with everything else. This is not, deliberately. The fork between the insurance path and the retail path is the highest-value decision in this business, and there is currently no step at which it is made. It is an outcome, not a decision.

B10 · The fork Should be detected on the phone at B3 and confirmed on the roof at B6. Today it resolves by accident.
Is this an insurance job or a retail job?
Undefined. No criteria, no field, no step.

Average job value on the retail path. Price and scope set directly with the homeowner.
Why this one matters more than everything else on the page

The difference between the two exits is roughly $22,000 of average job value, and 19 of 25 low-value jobs were the owner's own. The person best at selling is making this call with nothing written down to make it against. This is not a criticism of judgment. It is an argument that judgment exercised a hundred times without a criterion cannot be taught to anyone else, cannot be checked, and cannot be improved.

The measurement rail

This is the layer that turns a picture into a management instrument. Leading indicators are the ones still open to influence. Lagging indicators confirm what already happened. A good scorecard pairs each leading indicator with the lagging result it produces. Read the right-hand column downward before reading anything else on this page.

The leak rail

Fail points, Map B Each one sits at the step where it bites, with the evidence attached.
F1  B1 One phone, one personGuy answers every call and runs every site visit, which means the hours he is least able to pick up are exactly the hours he is doing the work that generates the next call. Nobody knows what happens to a call when he is on a roof. Spend that produces more calls than one person can answer converts to nothing, and a homeowner with a leak calls the next contractor on the list.
F2  B3 "Minimum" is not written downThe questions vary by call and the answers land nowhere a report can read. This is the step where the insurance fork should be detected and where date of loss should be captured. Neither happens systematically. It costs nothing to fix and it changes the value of everything downstream.
F3  B4 The funnel has no numeratorAppointment outcome is blank on 24 of 24 records. All of them. Set-to-sat and sat-to-sold are the two core conversion metrics of a roofing sales operation, and neither can be computed. Without them there is no way to tell whether a shortage of sales is a marketing problem or a selling problem.
F4  B6 The three questions have no standard answerThe three things customers ask most, in the owners' own order: how long will this job take, are you an employee, and how long is the warranty and what does it cover. The second is a trust question created by the all-subcontractor labor model. The first cannot currently be answered from data, because 48 of 58 completed jobs were stamped done on a single day, erasing cycle time. The most common question their customers ask is the one their own system can no longer answer.
F5  B7 Photographs that no report can seeNone of the three connected tools contributes a single field that any Contractors Cloud report can aggregate, filter or measure. Stated carefully, because it matters: absence from a report export is not proof the tools go unused. What is proven is narrower and still costly. Open one recent project and see what appears. Five minutes settles it.
F6  B8 Measurements that live in another applicationEagleView is confirmed purchased and its output confirmed absent from the CRM. The account is real and reports have been pulled. Nothing measured has landed anywhere a report can read, so the moment anyone wants squares they open a second application.
F7  B9 A good template nobody finishesThe milestone template is ten stages and somebody thought about it properly. Across 230 milestone rows on 23 records there have been seven completions in 26 months, and all seven are on stage one. Every stage from "Sign Deal or Cancel" onward has been completed zero times. This is not a template problem.
F9  B11 A named role that appears nowhere in the processKurtis Tesmer's job title is "Supplementor." He appears in the system with zero claims, zero tasks, zero appointments and zero projects as rep. A named specialist sitting directly on the fork worth $22,000 of average job value, with no recorded participation in it, is the most under-examined thing on this map.
F10 at B14 · Good news wearing bad news clothes

The reasons they lose are already known, and price is barely one of them. All 34 cancellations carry a real, chosen reason, because the cancel dialog demands one. This is the only field in the system that is filled completely with differentiated values, and it is a small goldmine that is already working.

Summary

The three worst leaks

If nothing else from these two maps survives the meeting, these three should.

1
Attribution is dead at the intake field
159 of 161 leads are coded "Referral," with two exceptions in 26 months, and 62 of 62 open leads carry the same single value. Lead source is captured and never used. No channel can be measured, so no marketing spend can ever be judged, so every downstream decision in this engagement runs through one field that takes an afternoon to fix. This gates everything.
2
The most valuable decision in the business is not a step
Insurance-scoped jobs average $41,048 against $18,565 retail, a swing of roughly $22,000 per job, and 19 of 25 low-value jobs were the owner's own. The fork has no criteria, no field, and no moment at which it is consciously made. It is currently an outcome rather than a decision.
3
The conversion funnel has no numerator
Appointment outcome is blank on 24 of 24 records. Milestones have been completed seven times in 26 months, all seven on stage one. Cycle time was erased when 48 of 58 completed jobs were stamped done on a single day. Set-to-sat, sat-to-sold, proposal-to-close and days-to-close are all uncomputable from their own system, which means nobody can currently tell whether a shortage of sales is a marketing problem or a selling problem.
The through line

All three leaks are the same leak. Every one of them is a field that is not required. And the business has already proved, in its own data, that it will fill a field the workflow forces: 34 of 34 cancellations carry a real reason because the cancel dialog will not close without one. Nothing on these maps asks the owners to work harder or to buy anything. It asks four fields to become required.

Note

Noted, out of scope

EagleView is confirmed purchased and its output confirmed absent from every Contractors Cloud report. It appears on Map B as fail point F6 because that is the step where it bites the sales process. The wider question of the tool stack belongs to a different workstream and is not pursued here.
