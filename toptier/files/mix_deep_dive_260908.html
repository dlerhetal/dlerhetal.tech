1

Market mix: who pays for the roof

What it is now
Across 77 contracts and $2,441,252 of signed work since June 2024, insurance-scoped jobs are 45 contracts and 75.7% of your revenue at an average of $41,048. Retail is 32 contracts and 24.3% of revenue at an average of $18,565. Contractors Cloud
What changed
Insurance was 72% of the jobs you signed in 2024. It is 36% of the jobs you have signed in 2026. The share of your work that a carrier pays for has been cut in half in two years. Contractors Cloud
What it means
An insurance job is 2.2 times the size of a retail job. When the mix moves toward retail, your average job falls, and the number of jobs you must sell every month to cover the same overhead goes up. It feels exactly like margin falling. It is not margin. It is mix.

Why the mix moved: the weather, and it is on the federal record

The belief in the building is that carriers stopped paying as well. Your own numbers say the opposite, and the real answer is better news. The average insurance job you signed went up every single year: $45,079, then $64,326, then $85,569 on the jobs over $25,000. Contractors Cloud Carriers paid more per claim. There were simply far fewer claims to have.

The August 20, 2026 event is preliminary and it is live. Those nine reports are raw National Weather Service local storm reports and the sizes get revised during federal quality control. They landed in the exact footprint that produced the highest tickets in your history. Your Crosslake average job is $82,597. You have nine past customers in that corridor and you have not called them in twenty three months.
Market mix, the verdict
Your revenue is storm revenue, and the business did not know that about itself. Two nights in 2024 built the book. 2025 gave weather without severity. 2026 has given nothing. Your book changed shape by exactly as much as the sky did. That is a fact about the weather, not a failure by anybody in this room, and it means the lead program has to do two jobs at once: build demand that does not need hail, and be ready to move the day hail arrives.
2

Product mix: what you actually sell

What it is now
You are a roofing company. Roofing is 90.4% of every dollar invoiced. Siding is 5.4%. Remodeling is 0.0% of dollars and does not appear on the 2025 tax return at all. It is in the company name and nowhere else. Contractors Cloud Form 1065
What changed
Nothing, and that is the point. In twenty seven months, siding sold on its own exactly twice, for $29,755 total. Every other siding dollar rode along on a roof.
What it means
Siding is not a second business line. It is an attachment rate. A job that carries siding averages $37,787 against $29,502 for a roof alone. That is a 28% bigger ticket for one extra sentence in the estimate, not a siding campaign, not a siding truck, not a siding page on the website. Contractors Cloud
Product mix, the verdict
One product, one attachment, one dead name. Sell roofs. Attach siding to every roof estimate as a line the homeowner can decline. And decide on purpose whether "and Remodeling" stays in the company name, because right now the name is promising something the business does not sell.
