Top Tier commercial leave behind

One printed piece for owners of more than one building. It is not a mailer and it carries no hook for a stranger, because it is handed to somebody who has already spoken to Guy. Its job is to still be on the desk in three weeks. The front makes the argument, the inside proves the craft, and the back is a blank roof record that is worth keeping on its own.

Two render assets, one per side of the sheet. Everything on both sides is drawn or set in type. Nothing loads over the network. The dashed red and navy lines you can see below are proof guides that sit outside the exported artwork and never print.

Format, and why

  • Flat trim 17 x 11 in, folded to 8.5 x 11 in, one vertical fold at the center. Tabloid duplex plus one fold is a job every copy shop and every digital press runs without a conversation.
  • A folded piece reads as a document, not a flyer. A flyer gets binned. A folded piece with a cover gets set down, and a cover buys the inside spread the room the cutaway drawings need to actually read at arm's length.
  • The back is useful on its own. It is a blank roof record, one row per building. That is the reason the piece survives on a desk: an owner with a dozen buildings does not have roof ages on one page anywhere, and the sheet that holds them gets kept.
  • Stock: 80 lb cover, matte, scored before folding. Cover weight cracks on an unscored fold. 100 lb text also works and folds without a score if the run is cheap.

Imposition

  • Side A, outside (commLeaveBehindOutside): back cover to the LEFT of the fold, front cover to the RIGHT of the fold.
  • Side B, inside (commLeaveBehindInside): one continuous spread, inside left panel to the LEFT of the fold, inside right panel to the RIGHT.
  • The two sides back up half for half: the back cover is the reverse of the inside left panel, and the front cover is the reverse of the inside right panel. In office printer terms that is the duplex setting where the sheet turns about its 17 inch edge. Pull one sheet, fold it, and confirm before the run.
  • Bleed 0.125 in on every edge, so the artwork file is 17.25 x 11.25 in. Safety a further 0.125 in inside the trim. Nothing that has to survive the knife sits outside it, and nothing critical sits within 0.25 in of the crease.
  • Files to hand the printer: the two ..._17.25x11.25in.pdf pages. The text in them is vector. The _300dpi.png rasters are 5175 x 3375 and are the fallback.

What is checked, and what has to be swapped

  • MN license BC807923 is live text on both printed sides: on the front cover twice, on the back cover, and on the inside spread. Closed, the two panels facing out both carry it. Open, the spread carries it. Minnesota Statute 326B.87 subd. 2 requires the license number in advertising and names brochures explicitly. The phone number is live text too, and the logo raster is the cropped wordmark with no phone or license lockup baked in, so neither prints twice.
  • No insurance language beyond the verbatim quote. The Insurance Federation of Minnesota is quoted word for word with the full citation, and the approved line follows it. Nothing on the piece interprets a policy, advises on coverage or mentions a deductible, per MN 325E.66 subd. 1(a)(3) and 72B.02 subd. 6(2).
  • One photo slot, on the inside spread. It is the only element that must be replaced or deleted before this goes to a press. Every other element on the piece is drawn or set, so the piece is complete with zero photographs.
