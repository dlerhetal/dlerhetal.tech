Are we still on track for $1.2M? No - behind, and the calendar is working against us

$700,000 is in the book with about two-thirds of the year gone. The problem is not just the gap; it is when the gap has to close. Roofing money is made in the summer, and the summer is already inside the $700,000. Only the fall tail is left.

Hitting $1.2M means selling 46% faster every month from here to New Year's than the company has averaged all year - and doing it in the back quarter, after the peak install months. That is why the honest answer is "stretch," not "on track."

The non-obvious point: seasonality does not rescue this number. In April or May, a back-loaded season would be an argument that the big months are still ahead. In September they are behind you - the summer install peak is already inside the $700K. To reach $1.2M the fall would have to be ~42% of the whole year's sales, which is above anything in this company's short record (see the seasonal box below). $1.2M is reachable only in a genuinely strong, storm-driven Q4.

Outside factors: seasonality, weather, and the economy

None of this is enough history inside Top Tier to build a curve from - the company is barely two years old. So the seasonal shape leans on outside Minnesota roofing data, and the weather and economy are checked against public sources. Every claim below carries its source.

1. Minnesota roofing seasonality - real, and it works against a big Q4

  • The slow season in northern states runs roughly November through March; work concentrates spring through fall (jobnimbus.com/blog/roofing-slow-season).
  • An NRCA-cited figure puts winter revenue about 30% below the peak summer months for the average roofer (roofpredict.com).
  • Beacon Roofing Supply's 10-K reports its lowest sales and net income in the January - March quarter (deep winter), strong spring through early fall (SEC 10-K).
  • The best planned-replacement window in Minnesota is August through October, with mid-September to mid-October ideal (owlroofing.com, lutgensco.com).

What it means here: Q4 opens in peak season (Sep - Oct) and tapers hard into Nov - Dec. That is exactly why the base case front-loads the fall push into September and October and expects little from December. No source gives a hard month-by-month percentage split, so the curve is a defensible shape, not a measured table - labeled MODELED throughout.

2. 2026 hail - an active year that brushed the doorstep

  • 2026 was an active severe-weather year in Minnesota - first tornadoes and hail to 3 inches on April 13 (south-central MN) (MN DNR).
  • June 19, 2026 - the closest event: a cell developed on the Wright / Meeker / Stearns county border and drove 1-inch-plus hail through the Hanover - Corcoran corridor, immediately adjacent to St Michael and Albertville (NWS MPX, MN DNR).
  • The most damaging 2-inch cores landed in the north / northeast metro (NE Minneapolis, Fridley, Shoreview, Woodbury), not directly on Albertville (same NWS / DNR sources).

What it means here: a mild positive for the strong case - there is real storm-driven insurance work in the surrounding area to chase this fall. But the punishing cores missed the client's towns, and hail damage inside Albertville / St Michael / Otsego / Big Lake / Buffalo / Monticello could not be confirmed from public reports. So weather nudges toward the base-to-strong band, it does not manufacture the $500K.

3. Economy and housing - neutral, a slight positive

  • Twin Cities home prices stable to modestly rising - metro median ~$395,000, low-single-digit appreciation expected in 2026, tight supply (titlemark.com).
  • 30-year mortgage rates easing to ~6.1 - 6.2% in 2026, down from 7%+ in 2024 - 25 (neversperpichrealestate.com).
  • Harvard's remodeling index (LIRA) projects home-improvement spending growth easing from ~2.1% mid-2026 to ~1.6% by year-end - slow, steady, not a boom (JCHS Harvard).

Bottom line on outside factors: normal-to-modestly-favorable. Timing is the tailwind (Sep - Oct peak), the active hail year is a mild demand positive, and the macro backdrop is neutral. Nothing here changes the verdict - it supports the base-to-strong band ($1.0M - $1.09M) and argues against the low case, but nothing points to the record fall that $1.2M would require.

Methods and caveats - read before quoting any number

What is measured

  • Monthly deposits, both years - from Deerwood Bank business checking (acct ...1214), 19 consecutive statements Jan 2025 through Jul 2026, parsed to a monthly rollup (work/bank_monthly_rollup_260825.csv, forensics in work/bank_forensics_260825.md). All 19 months tie to the statement's own totals to the cent, and December 2025 ending cash ($248,279) ties to the filed Form 1065. This is the reliable revenue-timing signal.
  • 2025 full-year revenue $1,189,873 - filed 2025 Form 1065, gross receipts.
  • 2026 sold to date $700,000 and the $1.2M target - stated by the owners / Dale as of early September 2026.

What is modeled (and labeled as such throughout)

  • The seasonal curve used to redistribute annual revenue across months, and the 2025 "sold by early September" estimate built from it. It is a shape informed by Minnesota roofing seasonality plus the company's own deposit pattern - not a measured monthly bookings series.
  • The three year-end cases (low / base / strong) are scenario arithmetic on the $700K actual, not forecasts with a confidence interval.

The two caveats that matter most

  • CRM dates are not sale dates. Contractors Cloud records administrative / data-entry dates (bulk-entry sessions cluster timestamps; sale_closed_at is effectively unused), and contract dollars are contaminated by siding and gutter scope on multi-trade jobs. A naive "revenue by month" pull from the CRM is unreliable and is deliberately not the revenue timeline here.
  • Deposits lag sales. Cash arrives after the sale by the collection cycle - matched deposits in the record show 25 to 72 days of lag. So the deposit timeline is a proxy for when cash lands, which trails when work is sold by roughly one to two months. The $700K "sold" figure is ahead of the $550K "collected" figure for exactly this reason.
  • The $407K March 2025 deposit is set aside in the like-for-like comparison. It is a single unmatched deposit, larger than any job, partly non-revenue (transfer / retainage / batch), and it cannot be resolved without the general ledger. Including it distorts every 2025 pace figure upward.
  • Only ~2 partial years of history exist (business started 2024), so the seasonal signal is a thin sliver. The curve leans on outside Minnesota roofing data because the company's own history is too short to trust on its own.
