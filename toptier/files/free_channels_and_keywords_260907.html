Where keywords actually live — and the free channels behind the mailer

Every rule quoted below comes from the platform’s own documentation, checked on 7 September 2026. Where the industry repeats something the platform does not actually say, that is called out rather than passed along.

Start here, because it saves you the most time. Negative keywords are a Google Ads feature. They do not exist in ordinary search results, the map pack, your Google Business Profile, Meta, Nextdoor, Bing or Apple. If you are not buying ads, there is no negative-keyword box to find, and looking for one is time spent on a lever that isn’t there. One real exception is Local Services Ads, which has no keywords but does have three genuine exclusion controls — section 4.

2Three beliefs worth throwing out

“I should put keywords in my Business Profile.”

There is no keyword field. There is a name, categories, a services list, a description, hours and photos. Google names exactly three local ranking factors: relevance, distance and prominence.

“Everyone puts Roofing and the town in the business name.”

This is the single most dangerous thing you can do to the profile. Google’s own words:
“Including unnecessary information in your business name isn’t permitted, and could result in the suspension of your Business Profile.”
Google’s own example of a banned name is “Auto Service Experts.” That is the same shape as adding a service or a town to a real name.

“Top Tier Roofing & Remodeling” is safe because it is the actual name. “Top Tier Roofing Albertville” would not be. The upside of the extra word is small; the downside is losing the profile that feeds the map pack.

The same rule reaches categories:

“Do not use categories solely as keywords or to describe attributes of your business.”

“The description is where the SEO goes.”

The services list is the only profile field with a documented matching effect:

“When local customers search Google for an offering you provide, that service may be highlighted on your profile.”

For the description, products and posts, Google documents no ranking or matching effect at all. Write the description for a human. Spend the effort on the services list instead — it is the field that actually does the work, and most contractors leave it half filled in.

3Two rules that will catch you out

A Business Profile post may not contain a phone number. Google calls it “phone stuffing”:
“To avoid the risk of abuse, we do not allow your post content to include a phone number. Instead, you can attach a ‘Call now’ button to your post.”
This is exactly the instinct to resist when posting about the mailer. Use the Call now button, which uses your verified number anyway.
Do not post your own questions and then answer them. Answering a real customer’s question is a proper feature and you should do it. But seeding your own questions falls under Google’s fake-engagement rule, which covers “content that has been posted from multiple accounts by or at the request of one person.”

Put your frequently asked questions on the website instead, where they are entirely yours and carry no risk.

4Local Services Ads: no keywords, but three real off-switches

This is the correction to the general rule, and it matters if you ever switch these ads on. You are not helpless about the work you get sent.

ControlWhat it does
Job typesToggles from a fixed list. Switch off the work you do not want to be called about.
Service area exclusionThere is literally a bar labelled “Exclude these service areas”. Search a county, city or zip code and remove it. A real geographic negative.
Broad searchThe closest thing to a negative keyword: a single on/off switch controlling whether you appear for category searches like “roofer near me”.
The trap, and it is the reason this section exists: you are switched ON for broad search by default, and turning off job types does not turn it off. Someone who unticks every job type they do not want, and stops there, still appears for general searches. Both switches, not one. Google’s own warning is that turning broad search off may reduce your lead volume — that is a trade, and it should be a deliberate one.
The good news if you are not buying these ads today: the two biggest levers in Local Services Ads that are not money are reviews and answering the phone — and those are the same two things that move the free map pack. Nothing you do now is wasted if you switch the ads on later.

5The free fortnight around a mail drop

Someone gets your card, is mildly interested, and types your name into their phone. Everything below costs nothing and exists so that what they find looks current and matches the card in their hand.

  1. Fill in the services list properly. The one field with a documented effect. Every service you actually offer, named the way a customer would say it.
  2. Check the name, address and hours are identical everywhere — Google, Facebook, Nextdoor, Bing, the website footer. Character for character. This is what “coordinated” actually means to a machine: same name, same address, same phone, everywhere.
  3. Post the same message on the Business Profile the week the cards land. No phone number in the text; use the Call now button.
  4. Post it on the Facebook Page and on Nextdoor too. Nextdoor lets a verified business post into chosen neighbourhoods free — use the neighbourhoods the cards went to.
  5. Add recent photos. Real jobs, recent dates. This is the cheapest credibility available and most competitors neglect it.
  6. Answer every review and every question. Responsiveness is a documented factor and it is visible to the next person reading.
  7. Claim the free Bing and Apple listings if they are not already claimed. Small effort, and Apple Maps is what every iPhone opens.
  8. Keep asking for reviews on every finished job. Reviews are the one input that moves the map pack, moves Local Services Ads if you ever run them, and costs nothing.

6Finding the words people actually use

You do not need a keyword tool, and the free ones will not give you town-level numbers anyway. You already have better data than a tool would give you: what people typed before they landed on your own site.

Google Search Console → Performance → Queries. That list is real searches by real people that reached your site, with sixteen months of history. Sort by impressions. Two things to look for:

  • Terms you appear for but rank badly on — high impressions, few clicks. Those are pages worth improving, because the demand is already proven.
  • Words customers use that you do not. If people search a phrase and you never say it on the site, say it — in normal sentences, not stuffed in.
