Top Tier Roofing and Remodeling

Where the Marketing Money Actually Is

Manufacturer and distributor funding for the direct mail campaign, after Owens Corning said no to co-op. Every claim below carries a source link and a verification label. Built 2026-08-31.

The answer

Matt is right that there is no percentage co-op fund at Owens Corning, and he is right about the whole category: almost nobody in asphalt roofing runs classic spend-matched co-op any more. What replaced it is purchase-based money, meaning you buy shingles, you get dollars back, and you spend those dollars on whatever you want, including a mailer. That reframes the question. The ask is no longer "will you fund half my ad," it is "how many dollars per square am I leaving on the counter, and who files the paperwork for me."

On that basis there are four doors, and three of them are open to Top Tier today at current size with zero certification:

  1. CertainTeed Contractor Cash Back pays $1.50 to $2.50 per square on shingles with no tier, no certification and no volume floor. Register, submit invoices, get a Visa card.
  2. IKO ROOFPRO Advantage pays $1.50 per square on named shingle lines with a 500 square per year minimum and a signed agreement.
  3. ABC Supply in Rogers, nine miles up 101, runs the actual mailer as a program: single family, owner occupied, homes ten years and older, inside a zip code or a radius, and they hand back the list of who received it. They also auto-file the manufacturer rebates above on your behalf.
  4. Owens Corning is not closed, it is mislabeled. OC's own network page says members earn promo funds on qualifying purchases that can be applied to marketing materials and lead generation tools, and can now be moved to a checking account or debit card. That is money. Preferred status, which the two year clock cleared in May 2026, adds a free custom design service that explicitly does direct mail.

The single highest value move is the distributor, not the manufacturer. ABC Supply is the only place where the mailer and the money sit at the same counter, and where somebody else does the rebate filing for a shop with no back office.

1. The Owens Corning premise check

Before going elsewhere, test what "we don't do co-op money" actually means. It turns out to mean two true things and one false one.

True, and it is money: OC network members earn promo funds on purchases Verified

From the Owens Corning Roofing Contractor Network page, under the heading "Total Rewards, Totally Awesome," OC's own words:

"Earn cash back on qualifying product purchases and apply where and when you want it. Members can earn promo funds on qualifying Owens Corning Roofing purchases that can be easily applied to purchasing valuable marketing materials, branded merchandise and lead-generation tools. For additional convenience and immediate access, funding can now be transferred to your existing checking or savings bank account or debit cards."

The same page lists three member benefit headings: "Grow Your Business with Owens Corning," "Customized Marketing Materials," and "Reimbursement Claims." So there is a reimbursement mechanism inside the program. What there is not, anywhere on the public page, is a published percentage, a cap or a spend match.

True: Preferred and Platinum unlock a free custom design service that covers direct mail Verified

Owens Corning Roofing Platinum and Preferred Contractors can also tap into our dedicated custom graphic design resource, Local Marketing Advantage (LMA), by taking their co-branded concepts to the next level."

OC's own Local Marketing Advantage flyer describes LMA as a "Unique, complimentary benefit that provides you with tailored or custom graphic designs for a variety of marketing collateral," and the collateral shown includes Direct Mail, door hangers, ads, job site signs and folders. The verdict, and what changes about the ask

Matt's statement is almost certainly accurate as stated. Owens Corning does not run a co-op advertising fund in the classic sense of reimbursing a share of an ad buy. But there is Owens Corning money, it is earned on purchases, and there is a free design resource gated behind Preferred. So the question to put to Matt is not about co-op at all.

What to ask Owens Corning:

  1. What is our current promo fund earning rate on qualifying purchases, in dollars or percent per square, and what is the balance today?
  2. What are the Preferred tier requirements in this territory, and what does a nomination take, now that the business has passed two years?
  3. Does Preferred change the promo fund rate, or only add Local Marketing Advantage and the extended warranty?
  4. Is Local Marketing Advantage still live in 2026, and does it still cover direct mail design?

The one requirement that rules a door out: GAF Master Elite Verified

GAF's 2026 Residential Program Guidelines, last updated 3/2/2026, set the annual Master Elite renewal minimums at 1,000 Rewards Squares (shingles only), 500 Warranty Squares under Silver and Golden Pledge, and 10 Learning Credits.

At roughly $1.19M of revenue, Top Tier's whole annual production is in the neighborhood of 1,000 squares. So Master Elite would require putting essentially every square they install through GAF, and registering half of it under enhanced warranties. That is not a marketing decision, it is a supply chain decision, and it is a bad trade for a shop that wants vendor flexibility.

The good news in the same document: GAF Certified has no volume bar at all. The published eligibility is state license where required, $1,000,000 liability insurance, and workers compensation insurance. Top Tier already clears all three with license BC807923. GAF Certified is free and reachable. It just does not come with co-op money, because GAF has none.

3. The distributors, which is where a shop this size actually gets paid

Manufacturer programs are built for volume. Distributor programs are built for the counter. Two things make distributors the better door here: they run the direct mail themselves, and they file the manufacturer rebate paperwork so a two person office does not have to.

AABC Supply, Rogers, Minnesota Verified

Branch: 22000 Industrial Blvd STE 100, Rogers MN. Open 7 a.m. to 4 p.m. Monday through Friday and, seasonally, 8 a.m. to 12 p.m. Saturday. That is roughly nine miles from Albertville.

Target Mail and Storm Postcards, the direct mail program

This is the closest thing in the whole search to the campaign Top Tier is already planning, run as a turnkey program:

  • Postcards are 6 inches by 9 inches, full color both sides, 14 stock designs, plus more product specific versions online.
  • Targeting, in ABC's own words: "The ABC Target Mail & Storm program sends personally addressed postcards to owners of single-family homes that are at least 10 years old." Targeting by household income or home value is available for an additional charge. Target a zip code or a radius around a specific job address.
  • ABC's stated purpose for the ten year filter: "Don't waste your marketing dollars mailing to apartment renters, owners of brand-new homes under warranty or homes outside your service area."
  • They hand back the list: "We'll provide you with a list of the names and addresses of the homeowners who received your postcard." That closes the loop into the CRM.
  • List only option: if Top Tier prefers to mail it themselves, the Target List program rents names for one time use. Minimum order is 300 names, delivered by email as an Excel file, labels on request.
  • Phone numbers can be included with the list, but only after registering with the National Do Not Call Registry and filing that registration with the program administrator.
  • Separate 4 inch by 6 inch storm postcards mail First Class within three business days of final proof approval. Three versions: two hail, one ice storm.
  • Administered for ABC Supply by The Weinstein Organization, phone 312-214-2915, email support@abcsupplyfreedomprograms.com, ordering at ABCSupplyFreedomPrograms.com.

4. What Top Tier could realistically get, ranked

Ranked by how likely the door is to actually open for a two year old, roughly $1.2M residential re-roofer with subcontracted crews and under 1% of revenue in advertising. Sequence, not a menu. All five are worth doing.

1CertainTeed Contractor Cash Back

Why it opens: it is the only program found with no tier, no certification, no volume floor and no annual fee. The published condition is simply that the program is for siding and roofing remodeling and retrofit contractors. Top Tier qualifies today.

What it pays: $1.50 per square on the mainstream Landmark lines, $2.50 per square on the premium lines, plus accessories including $4 per roll of WinterGuard, which matters in Minnesota where ice and water shield is on every job. Paid on a reloadable Visa.

First move: register at https://www.certainteed.com/contractorcashback to get a Contractor ID. Then either submit invoices directly or enroll with ABC or SRS so the distributor submits them.

Two conditions that cost money if missed: invoices must be received with the redemption form within 90 days of purchase date or they are rejected. And newly registered contractors may submit invoices dated up to 90 days prior to enrollment, so registering now captures the last quarter of buying rather than starting from zero.

What to ask: nothing. This one is self service.

2ABC Supply Rogers: Target Mail plus automatic rebate filing

Why it opens: nine miles away, and the program is built for exactly this campaign. Single family, owner occupied, homes ten years and older, inside a chosen zip or radius, with the recipient list handed back afterward. That last part is what turns a mailer into a trackable lead source instead of a hope.

What it costs: not published. That is the honest state of it.

First move: ask the Rogers branch for a Target Mail quote at the drop size Top Tier is planning, and ask them to confirm which manufacturer rewards they are already auto-filing on the account. Ordering runs through https://www.abcsupplyfreedomprograms.com/.

The list only alternative: if Top Tier would rather control the creative and the drop, the Target List program rents names at a 300 name minimum in an Excel file. That is a cheap way to test a route before committing to a full postcard run.

3Owens Corning Preferred, with the ask reframed

Why it opens now and did not before: the two year in business bar, which is the most consistently reported Preferred requirement, was cleared in May 2026. Top Tier already displays Owens Corning marks and already has a relationship with Matt. This is the shortest distance between where they are and a tier they were previously ineligible for.

What it gets: Local Marketing Advantage, a complimentary custom graphic design resource that explicitly covers direct mail, plus the exclusive authority to offer an Owens Corning extended warranty, which is a closing tool on every estimate. Whether Preferred changes the promo fund earning rate is not published.

First move: go back to Matt with the four questions in section 1. The change in the ask is the whole point: not "do you have co-op," but "what does a Preferred nomination take in this territory, and what is our promo fund balance."

The one thing to check before saying yes: the reported 50% Owens Corning product share commitment at Preferred. If that number is real, it constrains sourcing across half the book, and it needs weighing against the CertainTeed and IKO rebates above, which pay per square regardless of tier. Get the number from Matt, not from the internet.

4IKO ROOFPRO, Advantage now and Select as the stretch

Why Advantage opens: no minimum qualifications to join, and the $1.50 per square rebate requires 500 squares a year of architectural, performance or designer shingles plus a signed agreement. At roughly 1,000 squares of annual production, Top Tier clears 500 comfortably even splitting volume with another brand.

Why Select is the stretch worth naming: Select is the only tier found in this entire search that calls its money a marketing fund, described as funds for branded shirts, truck wraps and radio spots, on top of 0.25% on squares and 3% on accessories. The bar is 1,200 squares a year and at least 50% of residential shingles from IKO, plus two online courses. That is above current volume but not far above, and it is the closest genuine marketing fund to this company's size.

First move: ask the IKO territory sales representative for the Advantage agreement, and ask what the Select marketing fund is actually worth in dollars per year at 1,200 squares. That number is not published anywhere.

The tension to be aware of: IKO Select at 50% share and Owens Corning Preferred at a reported 50% share cannot both be satisfied. One brand share commitment fits in a book this size, not two.

5GAF Certified, free, and worth having for a reason that is not money

Why it opens: GAF's published eligibility for Certified is state license where required, $1,000,000 liability insurance and workers compensation insurance. Top Tier clears all three. There is no volume bar and no fee.

What it does not get: co-op money, which GAF does not have. Rewards points at 1.0% back, and per GAF's own mandated disclosure, "discounts on marketing tools." Discounts, not dollars.

What it does get: the badge, and per GAF's own advertising rules the permitted claim that "Only 7% of roofers in North America are invited to become GAF Certified Contractors." On a mailer, a second manufacturer credential next to the Owens Corning mark is credibility that costs nothing.

What to skip: Master Elite. The 1,000 Rewards Square annual renewal is effectively their whole production.

One compliance note that applies to every badge on every mailer: GAF requires the disclosure that certified contractors are not employees or agents of GAF and may receive benefits including loyalty points and marketing tool discounts. Malarkey, TAMKO and Owens Corning carry equivalent independent contractor disclaimers. Whoever lays out the mailer needs the current disclosure text from each brand whose mark appears on it.

Ruled out for this company, and why

  • Malarkey. No co-op, no rebate rate, no marketing fund published anywhere. Certification requires in person training and, for Premium, a Malarkey inspection of completed work. Effort with no money attached.
  • TAMKO Edge paid tiers. The cash back rate only gets interesting at the levels that charge $250, $1,000 or $2,500 a year. Paying an annual fee to unlock a rebate is the wrong shape for a company spending under $12,000 a year on all advertising.
  • Atlas. Free to join and harmless, but the reward is Atlas Bucks redeemable in a merchandise shop, and the earning rate is not published. Yard signs and folders, not mailer dollars.
  • GAF Master Elite. See above. The renewal minimum is the whole shop.
  • Beacon and QXO. No northwest metro branch, and the rebate terms are in flux post acquisition.

What could not be confirmed

  • Owens Corning promo fund earning rate. OC confirms promo funds exist and are transferable to a bank account. No percentage, no cap and no per square rate is published. Rep question.
  • Owens Corning tier requirements. The two year, three year, 50% and 80% share figures are secondary throughout, repeated by contractors and distributors, never stated on an Owens Corning page. Rep question.
  • ABC Target Mail pricing and minimums. Nothing published. Branch question.
  • Atlas Bucks earning rate. Nothing published.
  • TAMKO tier names. The fee and cash back columns extracted cleanly from TAMKO's own brochure, but the level names above them did not, so the pairing of Gold, Platinum and Diamond to $250, $1,000 and $2,500 is unconfirmed.
  • Whether any manufacturer requires creative pre-approval before a mailer runs. No program document found states a pre-approval step for advertising. GAF's guidelines instead impose content rules: mandatory disclosure text, mandatory hashtags on social posts, and a fixed list of permitted claims. That is a compliance requirement, not an approval gate. If Owens Corning's Local Marketing Advantage designs the piece, OC approval is implicit in the design service.
  • CertainTeed Roofer's Rewards dollar figures. From a 2022 brochure hosted by a distributor. Reconfirm before quoting.
  • A regional Minnesota independent distributor with its own program. Searched, not found. The upper Midwest independents were consolidated into SRS.
