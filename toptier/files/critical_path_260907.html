The critical path

Of everything this business could do this year, three things carry the money. This is what they are, who owns each one, what has to happen before the cash arrives, and why they are not three separate projects.

What “critical path” actually means here. Not a to-do list and not a wish list. A critical path is the short set of things where a delay delays everything — and, just as important, the things that can safely wait while you work them. Three cards are on the wall. Each one names a single problem, a single owner, a single number and a single date. If it is not on one of those cards, it is not what this year turns on.

2The path, drawn

Time runs left to right. The thing to notice is the arrow in the middle — that is what makes this a path rather than three lists.

Read it this way: reviews run the whole season and feed the residential track. Residential turns into cash in November. Commercial is slow on purpose — the work happens now, the money lands next spring. And the bar along the bottom is the only mechanism that keeps all three alive.

3Why the arrow matters more than the boxes

Reviews are not a third project. They are upstream of the first one. The review card sets its own exchange rate: three reviews produce one lead. So every week the review count stands still is a week the residential card gets harder, no matter what the mailer does.

That is what makes this a path. If these were three separate goals you could pick whichever felt easiest this week. They are not. The cheapest card on the wall feeds the most expensive one, and it is the only one that costs nothing but asking.

The honest read on the timing. One of these three pays this year. The commercial card is deliberately slow — the relationships built this autumn turn into work when next year’s budgets are set. Judging it in November would be judging it against the wrong clock.
One number on the cards is still unsettled and should be settled out loud. The residential card records today’s rate as one job from ten leads. The year’s target — 30 jobs at $5,000 — needs a much better rate than that against the lead goal on the card. The gap is not an error; it is the whole point of the year. But the rate you are aiming for should be written on the card, because everything downstream is priced off it: what a lead is worth, what a review is worth, and how much the mailer can justify costing.
