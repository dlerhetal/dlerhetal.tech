Getting your Claude the keys

One time setup, about five minutes, and then your Claude can save changes to the site instead of you clicking through a builder.

A person has to do this one

Create an application password for your account

An application password is a long code that lets a program sign in as you without ever holding your real password, and you can revoke it at any time without changing anything else.

  1. Go to https://toptiermn.com/wp-admin/profile.php and sign in.
  2. Scroll to Application Passwords.
  3. Name it something you will recognize later, for example claude, and add it.
  4. WordPress shows you six blocks of four characters. It shows them once.
  5. Paste it straight into your own environment file where your Claude reads its settings, as WP_APP_PASSWORD, alongside WP_SITE set to https://toptiermn.com and WP_USERNAME set to your WordPress username below.

Why a machine cannot do it: the code is generated inside a console you are signed into and displayed exactly once. Nothing can read it off that screen for you. Never paste that code into this page, into a chat message, or into any file that gets shared. It belongs in your environment file and nowhere else.

The words that are not allowed, and this is not a style preference

Minnesota law is specific about how a roofing contractor may talk about insurance. A contractor may inspect, document, measure, repair and replace. A contractor may not file, handle, negotiate, advocate or promise to ensure or get something covered, and may not offer to pay or waive a deductible. Those are claim handling, which needs a license this company does not hold, and the penalty runs to $10,000 per violation. Every piece of advertising also has to carry the license number, BC807923.

The instruction below carries those rules, so anything your Claude writes stays inside them. Keep them in any wording you write yourself.

Paste this into your Claude

What I want. First read the current homepage and the services page and tell me how the existing services section is built, so we extend what is there instead of bolting something new on. Then add the missing work to the homepage: roof replacement and repair, siding, gutters, and storm and hail damage, each with a short heading and two or three plain sentences, and each linking to the services page. Match the voice already on the site.

Hard rules for the wording, these are legal, not stylistic. Minnesota statute 325E.66 bars a roofing contractor from interpreting an insurance policy, from advising on coverage, and from advertising that it will pay or waive a deductible. Statute 72B.02 bars advertising as a public adjuster. So: use inspect, document, measure, repair and replace. Never use file, handle, negotiate, advocate, ensure or covered about insurance. Never say or imply that insurers drop homeowners over roof age, which is not true in Minnesota. Say nothing about what a policy will or will not pay. The license number BC807923 must remain visible on the page, which today it is, in the footer.

Adding another city, any time after today

The five towns on the step before this one are the first batch, and none of them exists on the site yet. This step is the general case: the same job, for any city you decide to add later, without anybody having to write a new instruction for it.

Read this first, because it is the one way this backfires. A stack of near identical city pages that differ only by the town name is what Google calls doorway abuse, and it is named in their published spam policy, not a grey area. Their own wording: doorway abuse is "when sites or pages are created to rank for specific, similar search queries", and one of the examples they list is "having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page". The same document names scaled content abuse, "when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users". Read either page at developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies. A penalty here does not land on the thin page alone, it lands on the site. Fifteen template fills are worse than three real pages.

What makes a city page a real page

Only one of these is something a machine can supply, and it is the last one. The rest have to come out of your own records, and that is exactly why they work.

  • Work you actually did there. A job on a named street or subdivision, what the roof was, what it turned into. One is enough, three is better.
  • Photographs from that city, your own, not stock. A house that is recognizably in that town does more than any paragraph.
  • Something true and local that a person who lives there would recognize: a storm and its year, the age and style of the housing stock, a landmark, the way a subdivision was built out all at once so the roofs all age together.
  • A reason the page exists beyond the town name. If you removed the town name and the page still read the same as the last one, it is not a page yet.
