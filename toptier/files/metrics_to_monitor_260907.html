The numbers to watch every week

Five numbers, counted the same way each week, on one sheet. Everything below is built from your own 2025 return and your own job book — there are no industry averages on this page.

Why five and not twenty. A number you look at once is a report. A number you look at every week is a habit, and only the second one changes anything. These five are the smallest set that tells you whether the work is working, and each one can be counted in under a minute from something you already have.

1The weekly scoreboard

Fill this in at the same time every week. The same day, the same person, every week — the value is in the run of them, not in any single week.

What to count Where the number comes from Why it is on the list This week
New leads, and the source of each one Contractors Cloud, count of projects created since last week The only number that tells you whether anything you spent money on is producing
New reviews posted Your Google Business Profile Reviews are the cheapest thing on this page and they compound
Jobs sold, and the dollar value of each Contractors Cloud, contract value on projects that moved to signed The result. Everything else is upstream of it
Inspections done Your own calendar or the crew The one number entirely inside your control — it moves before sales move
Marketing dollars spent The card statement, or the vendor invoices Without it the first number has no denominator
Lead source is the one that will not count itself yet. Today a new project lands on the default source, so the first column can tell you how many but not where from. That is fine while the work comes by name at the door. It stops being fine the week the mailer lands, because a postcard, a review, a map listing and an advertisement all arrive as the same thing — a stranger on the phone — and nothing about that call says which one paid for it. The sources have to exist in the system before the first card goes out, not after.

3The review chain, and why it is first

Reviews are the only line on this page that costs nothing and keeps paying. This is the path from a finished roof to the next lead.

Finished jobthe moment of most goodwill you will ever have
Ask, on sitewhile you are standing there, not by email later
Review postedpublic, permanent, and dated
Map visibilityreviews are a large part of who shows up locally
Stranger callsa lead you did not pay for

The target is 88 reviews. Not because 88 is magic — because it is roughly what the competitors who already show up in the local results carry. It is a position, not a score.

At $800 a review, the gap between where the count is now and 88 is not a vanity number. It is the largest piece of unclaimed value in the marketing plan, and it is the only one with no invoice attached.

Count reviews, not asks. Nobody can verify how many times somebody was asked, and a metric that cannot be checked stops being true within about a month. The posted count on the profile is the number.

The honest read on where the work comes from today: 83 of the 88 numbered jobs in the book came in by referral. That book is the business and it is working. The point of counting the five numbers above is not to fix it — it is to see the new channels clearly enough to know which of them is worth keeping.

5The two-minute weekly routine

StepWhat happens
Same day, weeklyOne person fills the five numbers in. It does not matter who, as long as it is the same person and the same day.
Read the runLook at the last four weeks together, never one week alone. A single week is noise; four weeks is a direction.
One questionWhich number moved, and what did we do differently? That is the entire meeting.
Keep the sheetsThe value arrives at week eight, not week one. A run of sheets is the only thing that can tell you whether the mailer worked.
