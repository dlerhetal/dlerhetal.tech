Images we need to find

Top Tier Roofing and Remodeling. Built 31 August 2026. Twenty one details. Each one needs a wrong and a right. Tick the box when it is shot.

Every detail here already has a cutaway drawing built. A real photo of the failure, beside a real photo of it done correctly, with the drawing explaining what the water is doing, is the finished piece. Either alone is half an argument. Almost every wrong one is sitting on the next tear off.
DetailGet the wrong oneGet the right one
1Drip edge, eave and rake
Tear off day. The wrong one is on almost every old roof.
Metal sitting tight against the fascia, or no drip edge at all. Water staining down the fascia behind the gutter.Drip edge standing off, water dropping clean into the gutter. Eave piece under the underlayment, rake piece over it.
2Synthetic underlayment
Any install, before shingles go down.
Old felt torn, wrinkled, or soaked and buckled.A clean synthetic run rolled out across the deck, printed side up, lap lines visible.
3Kick out flashing
Tear off. The rot shot needs the siding pulled.
The gap where a roof edge dies into a sidewall with no kick out, and the rotted siding or sheathing under it.A new kick out installed and lapped into the step flashing and the housewrap.
4Back caulking at penetrations
During install. This is only visible for about an hour.
A vent flange sealed on the front face only, or not sealed at all.The back and upslope sides of the flange sealed before shingles go over it.
5Ice and water shield at penetrations
During install.
A chimney, skylight or pipe with nothing but felt under it.Membrane wrapped around the penetration and up the curb, before shingles.
6Ice and water shield at the eave
During install, first thing in the morning.
The eave course with felt only, or membrane stopping short of the wall line.Membrane down at the eave, running past the interior face of the exterior wall.
7Ice dam
Winter only. First hard freeze. Cannot be staged or bought honestly.
Icicles and a ridge of ice at the eave, ideally with the melt line visible up the roof.A vented and insulated roof holding even snow with no ice at the edge.
8Attic ventilation
Attic, any time. Bring a light.
Soffit intake buried under blown insulation. A ridge vent or fan with no intake feeding it.Baffles holding the intake clear, and a continuous ridge vent.
9Step flashing
Tear off for the wrong one, install for the right one.
A bead of caulk where flashing should be, or one long bent piece instead of step pieces.Step flashing woven in, one piece per course, running up a sidewall.
10Chimney flashing
Tear off. Chimneys are the second biggest leak source.
Counter flashing smeared onto the face of the brick with sealant.Counter flashing let into the mortar joint, over the base flashing.
11Pipe boot
Every old roof has at least one. Easy find.
A cracked or split rubber collar, usually sun rotted on the south side.A new boot seated and shingled in correctly.
12Valley
Tear off.
A valley worn through, packed with debris, or shingled across with no metal.An open metal valley, or a clean closed cut.
13Nail placement
Tear off. Pull a shingle and shoot the back of it.
Nails driven high above the nail line, overdriven through the mat, or left standing proud.Fasteners seated in the nailing area, flush, correct count.
14Shingle offset
Tear off, on the deck, or from the ground with a zoom.
Joints stacking up course over course, making a straight channel down to the deck.A correct stagger.
15Starter strip
Tear off for the wrong one, install for the right one.
A three tab flipped upside down and used as a starter, or no starter at all at the rake.Purpose made starter along the eave and up the rake.
16Gutter apron
Tear off, or any rainy day.
A gutter with water running behind it, rotting the fascia board.Apron and drip edge each doing their separate job.
17Skylight
Install day, if a skylight is on the job.
A curb sealed with nothing but caulk.A proper head, sill and step flashing kit installed.
18Low slope and dead valleys
Porch roofs and dormer tie ins. Look for the stain.
Shingles laid on a pitch too low for them. A dead valley holding standing water or debris.The right product on the low slope section.
19Water arriving from inside the house
Attic. This is the ceiling stain that is not a roof leak.
A bath or kitchen fan duct dumping straight into the attic. Frost or staining on the underside of the deck.The duct run out through a roof or gable vent, sealed.
20Rotted decking
Tear off. Very common, very easy to get.
Soft, delaminated or blackened sheathing exposed after tear off.New sheathing in and nailed off.
21Where the water ends up
Ground level, any job, sixty seconds.
A downspout dumping right at the foundation.An extension carrying it away from the house.
Shoot tight and square on. Fill the frame with the flashing, not the house.    Get the wrong one before it comes off. Once tear off starts the evidence is in the trailer.    Note the job so the photo can be matched back to the record.
