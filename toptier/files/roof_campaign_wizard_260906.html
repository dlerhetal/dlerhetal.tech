The Roof Age Mailer, Start to Finish

Three postcards, 1,433 households, and how the CRM tells you whether it worked

Before you start

  • Who does this: Guy sets up the CRM. Whoever answers the phone needs five minutes of Step 6. Everything else is one person at a computer.
  • What you need in hand: a computer with a browser (this is not a phone job), the Contractors Cloud login with Corporate Settings access, and the print and postage invoice once it exists.
  • What you cannot do yet: the three in home dates are not set. The mail house confirms those. Until then, every date on this page shows as a blank you fill in once, on Step 5, and it fills itself in everywhere else.
  • The one step that is not reversible: sending a print file. Everything before that can be changed for free. See the red box on Step 3.
  • How long: about 20 minutes to read, about 10 minutes of actual clicking in the CRM.

Why these 1,433 and not a bought list

Every household was filtered from county parcel and permit records for roof age. The card says you need to know how old your roof is, and that claim is true for every address on the list. That is the whole advantage over a generic mailing list, and it is worth protecting.

How we know the roof ageHomesWhat that means
A permit was found and read398The roof age is a fact.
Full permit index searched, nothing found93Age taken from year built. Reasonable.
No usable permit index for that city942Age taken from year built. Not verified.
This is why the card asks a question instead of making a claim. For 942 of these homes we are inferring roof age from the year the house was built, and some of those roofs have quietly been replaced. A card that said "your roof is 21 years old" would be wrong at some of those doors. A card that says "you need to know how old your roof is" is true at every door, including the ones where the answer is "actually, it is fine."

The three cards

Same shape every time. Only the headline and the call to action change.

By the third one, the household should recognize the card before they read it. That repetition is the campaign, not laziness. The quotation, the citation, the logo, the phone number and the footer are identical on all three, down to the pixel.

The one line that is not decoration

Every card carries: "We can't help you with your insurance policy, but we can help you with your roof." Minnesota law (Minn. Stat. 325E.66) bars a roofing contractor from representing or advertising to represent insurance claims. The penalty is $10,000 per violation. The middle of the card quotes a public legislative filing word for word, in quotation marks, with its source printed underneath. Nobody adds a sentence explaining what that quote means. Quoting is allowed. Explaining is the thing that is not.

The filing itself is public and can be read here:

Why not the cheaper one

Marketing Mail saves $249. It is also, in the Postal Service's own words, deferred and not guaranteed, with a delivery window wide enough that drop 2 can land before drop 1. A sequence that arrives out of order is not a sequence. First Class also returns undeliverable mail for free, so after drop 1 the bad addresses come back and drops 2 and 3 stop paying to print and mail them. On a list this size that is roughly 43 wasted pieces per drop that clean themselves up.

$249 buys a campaign that arrives in the right order and a list that cleans itself. If a fuzzy in home date is genuinely fine, take Marketing Mail and keep the money.

Two things worth knowing before you order

  • Do not buy your own postal permit. The permit is $390 a year, which works out to more per piece than the mail house charges for the whole service. Use the mail house's permit.
  • Three different designs is three print jobs no matter how you order them. Ordering all three at once saves shipping, not printing. The only real batch saving, $345, exists if all three drops carry the same card, and they do not.

What the mail house needs from you

  1. The address list. It is a spreadsheet of 1,433 households, already standardized.
  2. An instruction to run CASS and NCOALink. This is address validation and change of address. It has not been done and it has to be done by someone licensed to do it, which is them, not us. This is what turns 1,433 into the real number.
  3. Which postage class. See the previous step: Presorted First Class is the recommendation.
  4. The three in home dates you want, so they can schedule the drops backwards from them.
Ask them one question early: how many days before an in home date do they need the file and the list. Every schedule on the next step depends on that number and only they know it.

The schedule

Set the three dates once, here. They fill themselves in everywhere else on this page.

Use the date the card lands on a kitchen table, not the date the file goes to the printer. Those are two weeks apart and only the first one can be compared against a phone call. Tuesday is the strongest in home day, and 14 days apart keeps the three drops from blurring into each other.

Two things to tell whoever answers the phone

  • Responses overlap. Calls from drop 2 are still arriving when drop 3 lands. Someone will call about a card they got two weeks ago while a newer one sits on their counter.
  • Some people call after three cards, not one. That is the design. Nobody should treat a late call as a worse call.

Set up the CRM, once

Ten minutes, one time, and then nobody maintains anything.

The short version: Contractors Cloud has no campaign feature. It was checked directly, not assumed. What it does have is a lead source record that already carries a start date, a cost, a description, and a live counter of how many jobs came from it. That record is a campaign object in everything but name, so that is where this lives.

Step 2. Paste this into the Description field

Postcard campaign to 1,433 households. Same list, three drops. Drop 1 in home DATE. Drop 2 in home DATE. Drop 3 in home DATE. Print and postage: $3,076. List: TopTier.MailerList.260831.csv

Do not make one lead source per drop

Three sources looks more precise and is actually worse, for a reason that has nothing to do with tidiness. This campaign is expected to produce somewhere between 7 and 43 jobs in total. Split three ways, each bucket holds single digits, which is smaller than the normal month to month wobble in the referral business. Three buckets of single digits is not three measurements, it is three anecdotes.

On top of that, all 1,433 households get all three cards. The third card works partly because they already saw the first two. Crediting a call to one drop would be wrong even if you could measure it perfectly.

Do not reuse this source next year

The cost field and the job counter are running totals that never reset. If a 2027 mailing rides on "Direct Mail 2026", the two years blend permanently and the cost per job becomes meaningless for both. Next campaign gets Direct Mail 2027. One source per campaign year, not per drop.

Step 3, optional, one minute

Put three all day entries on the CRM calendar, one per in home date, titled MAIL DROP 1 of 3, 1,433 homes. This is a reminder for the weeks it matters, not the record. The record is the description field in Step 2.

Step 4. Nothing else

Do not add a custom field. Do not create a project record for the campaign. Do not touch the Event field. Each of those was considered and each one breaks something. The worst is the fake project record: the project list is the thing being counted, so putting the campaign in it means the campaign shows up in every job count and every metric on the board.

When the phone rings

This adds zero steps to what the office already does.

Nothing new to remember. Lead Source is already a required field on every new job, and it is already filled in on all 296 records in the system. The entire change is that the dropdown has one more option in it.

The only decision the person on the phone makes

Did they mention getting something in the mail? Yes or no.

If yes, pick Direct Mail 2026 from the Lead Source dropdown. That is it.

Do not ask which card they got

They are holding one of three near identical postcards and they do not know which drop it came from. Asking makes the call worse and produces a guess, and a guess is worse than a blank because it looks like data. If a caller volunteers it, fine, ignore it. The system works out the timing on its own from the date the job was created.

The one field that matters more than it did yesterday

The site address, including the ZIP, typed accurately. We hold the list of all 1,433 mailed addresses. Any new job at an address on that list came from a mailed household whether or not anyone said so on the phone. That turns somebody's memory into evidence, and it costs nothing because the address is already being typed.

If they say a neighbor told them AND they got a card

Pick whichever they said first and move on. Do not invent a rule for it. The overlap gets handled later by the address cross check, and there is a note about it on the last step.

Finding out whether it worked

One export, four numbers, and an honest account of what they cannot tell you.

The report to run

Use the legacy project list export, the file that comes out named projectlists and then a date. It is the only export that carries the lead source, the creation date with the time of day, and the money on the same row, across every job status.

Do not use the report called "Leads"

It has a Lead Source column and a Created date and it looks perfect. It is a working queue, not a history: it only holds jobs still in the early stages, and it drops closed and cancelled records completely. A mail lead that actually sells disappears off it. Using it to measure the campaign would systematically hide the successes and make good mail look like bad mail.

The four numbers, in order

#NumberWhere it comes from
1How many calledRows where Lead Source is Direct Mail 2026. The CRM also keeps this live on the lead source record itself, no export needed.

Number 4 is the real evidence. A total can be a coincidence. Three bumps that each start a few days after a drop cannot be. If the mail is working, the shape shows it before the total does.

The cross check that costs nothing

Match the site address and ZIP on those rows against the mailing list. Every match is a mailed household that called, independent of what anybody said on the phone.

What this honestly cannot tell you

  • Which drop pulled. The response windows overlap. Report the overlap as unknown rather than assigning it, and remember the later cards are cashing in awareness the earlier ones bought.
  • That the card caused it. These households also see the website, the trucks, the yard signs, and they know people who used Top Tier.
  • The referral halo, and this is the biggest leak. Most of this company's work comes from referral. A mailed homeowner who mentions a neighbor's name gets logged as Referral, and the card that put Top Tier in their head is invisible. Every direct mail number here is a floor, never a total. The address cross check partly recovers it.
  • Anyone who did not call. No system measures the households that did nothing.
  • Nothing at all, if the dropdown is not picked. Every number above depends on one selection at the moment of the call, and it cannot be honestly filled in afterwards.
One number to read alongside it. Referral jobs will keep arriving during the campaign regardless. Read the direct mail count against the referral count for the same weeks, not against zero, or a normal referral month gets read as mail success or mail failure depending on the mood in the room.
