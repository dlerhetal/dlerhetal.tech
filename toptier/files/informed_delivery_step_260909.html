Informed Delivery, the free digital lift on mail you already paid for

Updated 9 September 2026. The Post Office account is open, the CRID is assigned, and Vistaprint's answer is the one thing still outstanding for drop 1.

Before anything else: this may not be possible on drop 1, and Vistaprint's reply settles it

Informed Delivery puts a color advertisement, with a clickable link, next to your postcard in the email the Post Office already sends the household every morning. It is free. It rides mail you have already paid for.

To switch it on, the Post Office has to be told which mailpieces are yours. That means two numbers: a Mailer ID and an IMb serial number range, plus permission to use the first of them. Both numbers are printed onto the cards by whoever puts them in the mail. Drop 1 went out through Vistaprint on 7 September, so Vistaprint holds both and Top Tier holds neither.

Vistaprint publishes nothing either way about handing them over. We looked. Their mailing services pages and their help center do not mention Informed Delivery, Mailer IDs or Intelligent Mail barcodes at all. That is not a no. It means nobody outside Vistaprint can answer it, which is why step 1 is the ask, and why that ask went in on 9 September.

The good news inside that. The Mailer ID printed on the cards does not have to belong to Top Tier. USPS's own portal guide says the number may be borrowed: "The MID can belong to the mail owner or a Mail Service Provider (MSP). The user of the MID must get approval from the MID owner prior to using a MID to conduct a campaign." So the ask is not a favor outside the normal process. It is the process.

Top Tier also needs its own Post Office business account. Whoever submits a campaign signs in with their own Mailer ID and CRID, even when the campaign runs on a borrowed number, so without that account a yes from Vistaprint cannot be used. That account is now open. Read it for what it is, though: an owned Mailer ID does not put a campaign on drop 1, because a campaign matches the number printed on the mailpiece, and on drop 1 that number is Vistaprint's. There is no workaround hiding in there.

The four facts the reply has to carry

  1. Was the order prepared as full service, or as basic? Ask it in exactly those words; it is standard mail preparation language and any production desk will know it. If drop 1 went out basic rather than full service, no campaign will show against it no matter what else is right.
  2. What Mailer ID is in the barcode on that order? A six digit or nine digit number that sits inside the Intelligent Mail barcode on every card. It will be Vistaprint's own number, not Top Tier's.

USPS matches a campaign to your cards on the Mailer ID and this range together, and since Vistaprint's Mailer ID is shared across many customers, the range is the only thing separating your cards from everybody else's.

Because the number belongs to Vistaprint, permission is part of the process rather than a favor. The fallback, and it is a good one

If Vistaprint will not hand over the Mailer ID, ask them to run the campaign instead

USPS allows the company that physically mails the pieces to set up and manage the campaign on the mail owner's behalf: "MSPs can initiate and analyze campaigns on behalf of the mail owner." This is often an easier yes than surrendering a Mailer ID, because handing out the number touches every other customer who shares it, and running one campaign does not.

The household still sees Top Tier's name, not Vistaprint's. The portal has a field called the Brand Display Name, and USPS's own instruction for it is explicit: "This name will show in the 'From' field in the Informed Delivery campaign presented to users. It must be the mail owner's name. For example, if an MSP represents Company123, this field should read 'Company123.'" So a Vistaprint-submitted campaign reads Top Tier Roofing and Remodeling in the morning email.

The one real cost. Whoever submits the campaign holds the numbers. USPS: "whichever organization inputs the campaign has access to the campaign data, management, and reports." So the open and click reports would sit in Vistaprint's portal and they would have to hand them over. Ask for that in the same message, in writing, before agreeing. A campaign that runs is worth more than a report you own on a campaign that never ran.

The trap that cost us time, so nobody repeats it

A plain usps.com consumer account is not the same thing and it does not produce a CRID. The everyday usps.com login, the one for tracking a package or holding mail, is a different system entirely. The business registration is at gateway.usps.com, and only that one issues the CRID the campaign portal signs you in with.

The person who submits an Informed Delivery campaign signs into the portal with their own Mailer ID and CRID, even when the campaign itself runs on a Mailer ID borrowed from the printer. The borrowed number goes into the campaign; your own numbers are how the Post Office knows who is sitting at the keyboard. So without this account, a yes from Vistaprint could not have been acted on. That is why it was opened alongside the ask rather than after it.

And the flip side, so nobody misreads this as the way around Vistaprint. Having a Mailer ID of your own does not rescue drop 1. A campaign keys on the Mailer ID printed on the mailpiece, and the number printed on drop 1 is Vistaprint's. An owned Mailer ID is worth having, and it is worthless on cards it was never printed on. This is not a workaround.

Ask your own Claude for it, or read it out of the vendor folder. Either has it in seconds. It also shows in the account or profile area of the gateway, next to the company name.

A CRID is a Customer Registration Identification number. It is how the Post Office identifies one business at one address, and it is assigned automatically the moment the business account is created. There is no form, no wait and no fee. It is one of the two numbers the campaign portal signs you in with; the Mailer ID in step 4 is the other.

One CRID per address, which is why a second registration is a mistake. If the business ever appears twice in the gateway, that means two addresses are registered. The one that counts is the CRID attached to the Albertville address.

What USPS says the picture should look like

Their guidance is short and it is worth following, because this image is competing with every other envelope in that morning's email:

  • "Sync the look of the Ride-along Image to the mailpiece." Somebody who is looking at the gray scan of your card should recognize the color image below it as the same card.
  • "Treat the Ride-along Image like a branded, clickable button with a clear call-to-action." It needs to look like something you press.
  • "Opt for a clean, simple layout." At 300 pixels across, only a few words survive.

The three things USPS lists as failures are a logo on its own, an image with no words on it, and anything that does not look clickable.

The second picture, and whether you need it. There is an optional representative image that replaces the gray scan with a color one. It is 780 pixels wide or 500 tall, same JPEG and 200 KB rules. It is required only for large flat mail, and a postcard is not that, so for drop 1 it is optional. Add it if the artwork is easy to resize; skip it if it costs you the deadline. The ride-along is the one that is always required.
What success looks like. A .jpg file on the desktop, no wider than 300 pixels and no taller than 200, under 200 KB, that reads as a button and looks like the postcard.

Step 8. Decide where the click goes

This is the difference between knowing the campaign worked and guessing.

Use one address that appears nowhere else

USPS's own advice: "USPS strongly suggests that mailers use a unique URL within the Informed Delivery campaign, one that is not used elsewhere in marketing materials or printed on the mailpieces, so that mailers can best measure results." If the campaign link and the link printed on the card are the same address, every visit blends together and neither number means anything on its own.

The best fact in the whole product, and most people miss it. When somebody clicks, USPS adds the Mailer ID and that card's own serial number onto the end of the address as parameters. USPS: the system sends "campaign Mailer ID (MID) and Serial Number information in the form of URL parameters when consumers click."
The serial number is the row that card came from in your mailing list. So a click does not just say somebody clicked; matched back against your own list, it says which house. USPS will never give you a name or an address, and question 18 of their FAQ says so directly, but you already hold the list, and the serial number is your own row number.

What Informed Delivery will not do

  • It will not give you an email address. USPS: "USPS cannot legally provide names or addresses of customers or Informed Delivery users to mailers." The only place an email address can come from is somebody typing it into your page.
  • There is a field in the report called Email_ID and it is not an email address. USPS describes it as a unique identifier for each mailpiece. Do not build anything on it.
What success looks like. A live https page, its address written down and typed into the campaign, and nobody using that address for anything else.

One choice on drops 2 and 3 that ends this before it starts

If a drop goes out as Every Door Direct Mail, Informed Delivery is impossible on it. EDDM pieces are not addressed to a person and carry no per piece barcode, so there is no Mailer ID and no serial number for a campaign to match against. That makes the choice between an addressed mailing and EDDM a campaign decision, not just a postage decision. Drop 1 was addressed, which is why it is still in play.

If each drop gets its own campaign, each drop needs its own serial range. USPS allows several campaigns at once as long as the serial ranges do not overlap. Since each drop is a separate Vistaprint order, each one should come with its own range anyway, which is another reason to ask per order rather than once.

What this is worth, said once and plainly

Around 44 percent of the homes on the list are Informed Delivery subscribers, so roughly 620 of the 1,411 households per drop get a second look at the card, in color, with a link, in an email they already open. Across three drops that is roughly 1,860 extra impressions for no media spend at all.

It will not carry the campaign on its own. It is a free second viewing of a card that was already paid for and already going to that door. The card still has to be worth reading, and the page behind the link still has to be worth landing on.
