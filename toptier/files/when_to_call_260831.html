Top Tier triage card, when to call

Two single sided cards on one trim size. Card one is the triage card: what to do today, what can wait, what belongs on a schedule, and the things that look like a roof leak and are not. Card two is the blank roof record, kept from the earlier piece because it was the part that was useful rather than persuasive.

Everything on both cards is set in type or drawn. Nothing loads over the network and neither card needs a photograph. The dashed red and navy lines below are proof guides that sit outside the exported artwork and never print.

Print specification

  • Flat size 8.5 x 11 in. Finished size 8.5 x 11 in, single sided, no fold.
  • Bleed 0.125 in on every edge, so each artwork file is 8.75 x 11.25 in.
  • Safety 0.125 in inside the trim. Nothing that has to survive the knife sits within 0.25 in of the canvas edge.
  • Resolution 300 dpi, 2625 x 3375 px. A vector PDF at the bleed size ships alongside it, and that is the file the printer gets.
  • Stock the two cards share a trim, so they gang on one sheet and print as a pair.

Card one · before you call a roofer

Four bands in urgency order. The fourth band carries the weight of the card, because a manager who is saved a wasted service call keeps the card.

Card two · the roof record

The one part of the rejected leave behind that was worth keeping, now its own single sided card on the same trim. It is a form, not an argument, which is exactly why it survives on a desk.

What is deliberately not on these cards

  • Insurance, in any form. No mention of a policy, coverage, a claim, a deductible or an adjuster appears anywhere on either card. Minnesota Statute 325E.66 subd. 1(a)(3) bars a contractor from interpreting policy provisions or advising on coverage without a chapter 72B license, and 72B.02 subd. 6(2) makes the advertisement itself the regulated act. A triage card gains nothing from raising the subject, so it does not.
  • Any promised response time, warranty term or service guarantee. None is on file, so none is printed.
  • Trades that are not confirmed. Roofing and siding only. Top Tier's own team page describes Jon LaRoque as a roofing and siding specialist and the site carries an LP SmartSide siding logo, so siding is sourced. Gutters, windows and insulation are named on the card only as places water comes from, never as work offered.
  • Photographs. Neither card needs one, so neither card has a slot for one.
