The message bank, and Facebook

A stocked shelf of what to say, sorted by what the customer is trying to find out, and then how to get it posted

Before you start

  • Two halves, two different jobs. The first half is a shelf of things to say. You read it, you pick from it, and you can come back to it for years. The second half is click by click inside Facebook, and it needs you at a keyboard.
  • The Facebook half needs you signed in to the Page as an admin. Not signed in to Facebook as yourself: signed in and then switched to the Top Tier Page, with administrator rights on it. If you are not an admin of that Page, everything from step 5 onward stops here until whoever is an admin either adds you or does the work themselves. That is not a thing this page can work around.
  • What you need in hand: a computer with a browser, your Facebook login, and the photographs you want on the posts. The shelf half works on a phone. The Facebook settings half does not, comfortably.
  • Nothing here is irreversible. A post can be deleted, a Page setting can be changed back. The one thing worth slowing down for is posting as yourself instead of as the Page, which is covered in its own red box on step 5.
  • How long: about 25 minutes to read the shelf, about 20 minutes of clicking in Facebook. The shelf is not a thing you finish, it is a thing you use.

What this is

A stocked shelf, not a script.

Everything on the shelf is something this company already says, or something a customer already said about it. It was pulled out of five places: your own FAQ sheets from the wall, the wall sheets from the room, the Google and Facebook reviews, the postcards, and the insurance and licensing work that decides what may be said at all.

It is organized by what the customer is trying to find out, not by where you would post it. That is deliberate. A homeowner worrying about their landscaping is worrying about their landscaping whether they are reading a postcard, a website page or a Facebook post. One entry off this shelf serves all three, which is why the shelf is worth more than a list of posts.

Nothing here is a phone script. These are written lines for things that get published. What you say to a person standing in their own driveway is yours, and this page has no opinion about it.

  • The channel tags say where an entry is fit to be used. Some entries are marked Internal only, which means they are true and useful but they are not a line to publish: your follow-up ladder, your commercial targeting, the things that must never be said.

What may not be said

Three rules. They are short, they are not opinions, and they apply to every entry on the shelf before it goes anywhere.

1. The verb test

In Minnesota, a roofing contractor doing claim work without a 72B adjuster license faces penalties of up to $10,000 per violation. The line between the two is the verb.

Roofing verbs. Safe.Claim verbs. Not yours.
inspectfile
documenthandle
measureadvocate
repairnegotiate
replaceensure
cover

That is the whole test. Read the sentence, find the verb, check the column. This page is not going to interpret the law for you and neither should anything you publish.

The sentence that keeps you on the safe side is already on every postcard: "We can't help you with your insurance policy, but we can help you with your roof."

2. Two things that may never be advertised

  • Paying or waiving a deductible. Minnesota 325E.66 subd. 1(a)(1) bars doing it and bars advertising that you will.
  • That an insurer drops homeowners over roof age. It is false in Minnesota. It is also the most common thing a storm chaser says at a door, which is a reason to be the company that does not say it.

What is safe is reporting what the industry itself said on the record. That is what the postcard does, and the source is public:

https://www.lcc.mn.gov/hcpi/meetings/20260205/Task-Force-on-Homeowners-and-Commercial-Property-Insurance-Final-Report

3. The review rule

Google's Rating Manipulation policy, live since 16 February 2026, bars a business from asking that specific content be included in a review. That means the ask may not name a staff member, may not name the service, and may not name the town. Naming the town is standard search advice, which is exactly why it is the trap.

It bans the ask, not the review. A customer who writes Guy's name because they felt like it has broken nothing, and the reviews already on the profile are safe.

The compliant ask names nothing: "tell us about your experience with Top Tier."

https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114

And one that is not a rule but is worth the same attention

The Minnesota Attorney General publishes, for homeowners, a list of contractor warning signs, and one of them is written material that carries only a phone number and no address. That is the argument for putting 11440 County Road 37 NE, Albertville and BC807923 on things. The words "locally owned" are just words. An address a stranger can drive to is a fact.

https://ag.state.mn.us/Brochures/pubHomeBuilding.pdf
