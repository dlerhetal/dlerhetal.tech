Start here

You are going to run the cash flow model yourself.

Thirteen screens. At the end you get one file: your own written procedure, plus a work order you can hand to Claude in your own terminal.

The cash flow model is the workbook that shows what cash Top Tier has now and what it will have every Saturday for the next thirteen weeks. It is not a report that gets finished and filed. It is an instrument. It keeps running after this engagement ends, because you keep running it.

Up to now it has been updated for you. This wizard hands it over. Nothing on any screen changes a file, sends anything, or touches the workbook. It asks you questions and writes them down.

Prerequisites. Have all of this before you start the weekly update, not before you start this wizard.

  • A laptop or desktop with Excel. This cannot be done on a phone or a tablet, and it cannot be done in Excel for the web. The workbook runs macros, and only desktop Excel runs them.
  • The workbook itself, named in the pattern TopTier.CFM.YYMMDD.xlsb, where the date is the Saturday that ends week one.
  • Your bank balance as of the Saturday just gone. Not today's balance. The closing balance on that Saturday.
  • The week's deposits and the week's checks, by name and amount.
  • About twenty minutes for the first run, and closer to ten once it is routine.

One thing needs another person: if the workbook lives in a shared OneDrive folder, nobody else can have it open while you update it. Message them first.

Every box in here is already filled in with the answer that matches how the model has actually been run. Most of the time you will read it, agree, and click Next. You are editing, not writing from scratch.

One number holds up the whole thing.

Everything else on thirteen weeks of forecast is built on the cash you actually had last Saturday.

The main sheet is the tab called Cash Flow & Cash Mgt. It runs left to right in weekly columns, and every column ends on a Saturday. Week one is the week just finished. Week thirteen is roughly three months out.

The tabs at the front of the workbook are template boilerplate from the people who wrote it. You never need to open them. The tabs you will use are these:

TabWhat you do on it
Cash Flow & Cash MgtThe main sheet. You type the beginning cash here and read the answer here.
Inflows - Non-ARMoney coming in. Customer deposits and payments, by name.
Outflows - Non-APMoney going out. Trade checks, materials, subcontractors, and the fixed card items.
RecurringThings that hit on the same day every month. Owner draws, and any named vendor on a subscription.

Yellow means type here. Orange means type here and everything depends on it. There is exactly one orange cell, and it is the beginning cash balance on the main sheet. Every other color is calculated. If a cell will not let you type in it, that is correct: it is a formula, and it is protecting itself from you.

What good looks like

For the week ending Saturday 2026-08-29 the model had forecast opening cash of 295,325.62. The bank actually said 294,388.00. The model was off by 937.62, which is 0.32 percent. That is a forecast you can make decisions on.

Read before opening the file

Three things to do before the workbook is on your screen.

1. Turn AutoSave OFF. If the workbook sits in OneDrive, Excel will try to save it continuously while you work. If Excel then crashes mid-update, OneDrive has already saved the half-finished version over the good one, and there is nothing to go back to. Open the file, look at the AutoSave switch in the very top left of the Excel window, and set it to Off. Do this every time. Excel resets it.

2. Close every other Excel window first. The workbook runs macros on open. If Excel is already busy with another file, the open can hang and you will be looking at a frozen window with no error message.

3. Make a copy before you touch anything. Right click the file, Copy, Paste into the same folder, and rename the copy with today's date on the end. The week roll you are about to do cannot be undone, and this copy is the only undo that exists.

When you open it, Excel will ask whether to enable macros. Say yes. The workbook cannot advance a week without them. If you say no, nothing visibly breaks, and the button you need simply does nothing when you click it.

You will know the file opened correctly when you see the tab strip along the bottom with the tab names from the last screen, and no yellow repair message across the top.

Gather the week before you open the workbook.

The update takes ten minutes if the numbers are already in front of you and forty if you go hunting mid-way.

Four things, in this order:

WhatWhere it comes from
Saturday closing balanceThe operating account, online banking, the balance at the close of the Saturday that ends week one. This is the orange cell.
Deposits in the weekThe same statement view. Customer name and amount for each one.
Checks and card charges in the weekThe same statement view. Trade checks, material suppliers, subcontractors.
What you expect next weekYour head and the job board. Which jobs collect, which material orders land.

Actuals beat forecast every time. Where a week has already happened, the number you type is what the bank says, not what the model guessed. The model earns its keep on the weeks that have not happened yet.

Not reversible

The week roll is one button, and it only gets pressed once.

Along the top of Excel there is a ribbon tab belonging to this workbook, named for the model. On it there is a lightning bolt labeled New Week. That button is the entire week roll. It moves thirteen sheets forward by one week at the same time.

Do not type the week ending date to advance the week. The date at the top of week one is a first time only entry, and it was set when the workbook was built. The button moves it. Anyone who tells you to retype it has just described a way to corrupt the model.

Pressing it twice destroys the forecast. There is no undo and no warning. If you press it and are not sure whether it ran, do not press it again. Look at the date on the week one column instead. If it moved forward by seven days, it ran.

The exact sequence

  1. Click the Cash Flow & Cash Mgt tab first. The button refuses to run from certain sheets, and the refusal message talks about something unrelated, so it reads like a different problem entirely.
  2. Click New Week.
  3. A Yes or No box appears titled Continue? asking whether you really want to advance, and telling you it is not reversible. Click Yes.
  4. A second box appears titled Forecast Model Advanced, saying all data has been moved up by one week. Click OK.

That second box is not proof it worked. If one sheet fails its own check, that sheet is skipped, the other sheets advance anyway, and the cheerful box still appears. So after you click OK, look at the date on the week one column and confirm it moved forward seven days. If it did not, stop and go back to the copy you made.

What the button did: it moved this week's invoices into the prior week section, cleared the current week ready for your new numbers, shifted receipts, expenses, the bets and the credit lines forward one column, and emptied the beginning cash cell. That last one is deliberate, and it is the next screen.

The orange cell

Type the bank balance. Then say where it came from.

On Cash Flow & Cash Mgt, just under the week one date, there is a single orange cell for beginning cash. The New Week button emptied it on purpose, because it is the one number that must be re-sourced from the bank every single week rather than carried forward by arithmetic.

Type the closing balance of the operating account on the Saturday that ends week one. Not the available balance, not today's balance, not the balance minus checks you know are coming. The closing balance.

Why this cell and no other: every one of the thirteen week endings is calculated from it. If it is wrong by ten thousand, the whole forecast is wrong by ten thousand, and nothing on the sheet will look broken.

Worked example, your own numbers. On the week ending 2026-08-29 this cell took 294,388.00, the close of business Saturday on the operating account. The model had forecast 295,325.62 for that same cell a week earlier. The gap between those two is the score on the next screen but one.

Money in goes on Inflows - Non-AR, by name.

One row per customer, not one lump per week.

The row across the top of that sheet is the weekly total of customer deposits and payments. Underneath it you can add named rows, and you should. A row that says 30,000.00 tells you nothing next month. A row that says the customer name and 30,000.00 tells you which job that was.

The columns run left to right in the same weekly order as the main sheet, so the column you type in is the week the money actually lands, or the week you expect it to land.

The weekWhat goes in it
Week one, already finishedWhat the bank actually received. Actuals.
Weeks two and threeNamed jobs you expect to collect, with the customer's name on the row.
Weeks four onwardWhat the season says. This is where the model's own history does the work for you.

Never enter the same money twice. The workbook has a second, automatic way of forecasting collections from sales history. That lane has been deliberately switched off for this model so that the only source of incoming cash is what you type. If a row on the main sheet labeled Received on A/R ever shows a number in the same week as your typed deposits, stop. Something has turned that second lane back on and the week is being counted twice.

Money out is split three ways, and one of them is a trap.

WhereWhat lives there
Outflows - Non-AP, the top rowsThe three big variable buckets: trade checks, materials, subcontractor labor. These move with how much work you did.
Outflows - Non-AP, the rows beneathThe flat monthly items that never change amount. These are constants and they get retyped after every week roll, because the roll clears them.
RecurringAnything that hits the same day every month. Name, how often, which day, how much. Owner draws live here.

The trap: never model the credit card payment. One row that says the card payment was 27,826 tells you nothing you can act on. You cannot cut it, and you cannot see what is inside it. Put the vendors in by name instead, one row each, and put the loose variable spend like fuel and meals into a category row with no vendor name. Then make sure the card payment row itself is gone, because tracking both counts the same money twice.

Where a vendor's price went up, forecast the new price, not the average. A vendor that started at 225 a month and now bills 300 goes in at 300. The average is a number that describes the past and predicts nothing.

Your own proportions, from the twelve banked months

Over the twelve complete months on the bank record, out of every dollar received: about 67 cents went out as trade checks, about 17 cents as materials, about 2 cents as subcontractor labor. If you have no better information for a future week, those are the shares to use. If you do have better information, yours wins.

Two things that make the model worth opening.

1. The bets, on the UDEs tab

An Undesirable Effect, which is what the letters stand for and what the cards on the wall are, is a dated bet that makes or saves a specific number of dollars, with one owner's name on it. The tab carries the description and the weekly dollars.

The three signed cards total 360,000. Putting them on this sheet is what turns them from a wall into a forecast: the model then shows you the cash position with the bets landing, next to the position without them.

One warning on that sheet. Some of its cells are merged, which in Excel means the box you see is several cells joined together. If you type into one of those and the value seems not to stick, click the box, look at the address in the top left corner, and type into the cell it names. This is an Excel behavior, not a broken model.

2. The score

Near the bottom of the main sheet a single percentage appears. It is not an opinion and nobody types it. It is this week's actual opening cash divided by what the model forecast for this week a week ago.

ReadsMeans
90 to 110 percentThe forecast is good. Make decisions on it.
Under 90Less cash arrived than expected, or more went out. Find which before you plan anything.
Over 110More cash than expected. Usually a deposit that landed early, or an expense that has not hit yet and is still coming.
ZeroThere is no prior week to score against. Normal on the first ever run, and normal nowhere else.

On the week ending 2026-08-29 it read 99.68 percent, which the sheet displays as 100.

Every week, before you save

Six checks. None of them take longer than a few seconds.

All six exist because each one has failed silently at least once somewhere, with nothing on screen to say so.

#Look atIt is right when
1The thirteen week ending dates across the top of the main sheet.Every one of them is a Saturday, and they run seven days apart with no gaps.
2The week one date specifically.It moved forward seven days from last week's file. If it did not, the roll did not take.
3The orange beginning cash cell.It matches the bank, to the cent, for that Saturday.
4The main sheet row labeled Received on A/R.It is zero in every week. If it is not, incoming cash is being counted twice.
5The score near the bottom of the main sheet.It sits between 90 and 110 percent. Outside that, read the next screen before you save.
6The Seasonality tab, a quick scroll.No red error text anywhere on it. If there is, the fix is on screen twelve.

Then save, and rename. Save the file with the new date in the name, in the pattern TopTier.CFM.YYMMDD.xlsb, where the date is the Saturday that ends the new week one. Last week's file stays exactly where it is. You never overwrite, so you always have every week you have ever run.

Turn AutoSave back off if Excel turned it back on. It does that. Check the top left corner before you close.

A number that looks wrong is information. Do not type over it.

The first instinct is to correct the answer. The answer is calculated, so correcting it destroys the calculation and hides the thing that was actually wrong.

What you seeWhat it usually is
The score reads well under 90 percent.Cash came in slower than forecast. Look at the deposits you predicted for last week and find which one did not land. That is a collection conversation, not a model problem.
The score reads well over 110 percent.An expense you forecast has not been paid yet. It is still coming. Do not spend the difference.
Week thirteen shows cash you do not believe.Week thirteen is the furthest out and the least certain, and it is the week most likely to be empty because it only just appeared. Check it has any forecast in it at all.
A row reads zero and you expected a number.The most common cause is a missing date somewhere upstream. A dated item with no date does not land in any week, and it does not raise an error. It simply is not there.
Red error text on the Seasonality tab.A month with no deposits at all breaks a running total on that tab. See the next screen. It does not affect the forecast.
A cell will not let you type in it.Correct behavior. That cell is calculated. Find the yellow cell that feeds it.

Never unlock a protected sheet, and never go looking for the password. Every number you need to change is reachable in a cell that already lets you type. If one genuinely is not, that is the moment to stop and ask rather than to force it.

The recovery is always the same. Open the copy you made before you started, and do the week again. That is the entire reason the copy exists, and it costs ten minutes rather than a week of forecast.

One cleanup before the workbook goes on a screen in front of anyone.

On the Seasonality tab there is a row of monthly sales for the current year. If any month in that row banked nothing at all, and February 2026 banked nothing at all, a running total on that tab breaks and prints red error text across four blocks of the sheet.

It is cosmetic. The forecast does not depend on that row and every number on the main sheet is unaffected. But red errors on a projector read as a broken model, and nobody in the room will hear the explanation.

The fix, and only this fix works. Clear the whole current year monthly row. Leaving the empty month blank does not fix it. Typing zero into it does not fix it. Only clearing the entire row does. Put the values back after the meeting.

Before you clear it, copy the row and paste it into a blank sheet so you have the values to restore. Then clear the row on Seasonality.

This is a monthly job, not a weekly one. That row takes one number per completed month. You are not touching it in a normal Monday update.
