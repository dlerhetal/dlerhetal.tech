Two things have to exist first

Neither of these is a habit. They are settings, they are inside your account, and nothing on this page works until they are done.

  • The lead source list has to be built. Today the catalog holds two entries. Asking anyone to pick a lead source out of a two item list is asking them to pick between two wrong answers. The list has to contain the ways work actually reaches you before the field can mean anything. Outside software cannot add these; they are created in your account, by you.
  • The estimated expense defaults have to be turned off. The system currently applies 65.00% estimated expenses and 10.00% payouts to every contract. Those are company settings, not calculations. While they are on, every margin the screen shows you is the default looking back at you.
One rule that applies to all five. None of these can be filled in later from memory, and none of them can be filled in by anyone outside the company without guessing. History stays as it is. Every one of these fields starts producing value from the next record forward, and never before. That is why the moment matters more than the field.

The five disciplines

DISCIPLINE 1Lead source, before anything else is typed
The field
leadSource, set to required so the record cannot be saved without it
The moment
At record creation. The first screen, before the address, before the name
Who holds it
Whoever answers the phone and creates the record
What breaks
Nothing can be attributed. The value of a review, a mailer, a yard sign or an ad becomes an opinion instead of a measurement, and the marketing budget gets set by argument
VERIFIEDThe catalog holds two entries, so the field has had nothing useful to hold. Referral work does not need this field: a referral announces itself by name at the door. The new channels do not. A mailer, a review, a map listing and an ad all arrive as a stranger on the phone, and the only record that they came from anywhere is the one made at that moment.
INFERENCEThe system already proves the fix works. Cancel Reason is filled on 34 of 34 records, because the dialog will not close without it. Required fields get filled. Optional fields do not. This is a settings change, not a willpower problem.
INFERENCEThis is the highest value field in the system, and the only one on this page that is completely unrecoverable. A cost can be found on a bank statement a year later. A completion date can be reconstructed from a photo. Where a lead came from exists only in the head of the person who took the call, and only for about a day.
DISCIPLINE 2Close the job when it closes
The field
Job status, advanced to complete, which sets the completion date dateDone
The moment
The day the crew leaves the roof, standing at the finished job
Who holds it
Whoever is on site at completion
What breaks
Cycle time, seasonality, and how long a job actually takes, all three unmeasurable. Also, any report pulled off the system describes a business one or two stages behind where you really are
VERIFIED48 of 58 completed jobs were closed on a single day, 2026-07-23. The completion date field records the day of the cleanup sweep, not the day the roof was finished, so it cannot be used for anything. That is the entire reason nobody can tell you your average job duration, which is the first question most of your customers ask.
INFERENCEOf the five habits, this is the only one that produces a number the customer wants to hear. Once a season of real completion dates exists, the answer to "how long will this take" stops being an estimate and becomes something the system can back up.
DISCIPLINE 3Costs land on the job
The fields
materialCost and laborPayout, posted against the job, not the month
The moment
Two moments: when the supply house invoice arrives, and when the crew check is written
Who holds it
Whoever orders the material, and whoever writes the check
What breaks
Job margin does not exist. The software fills the hole with an assumption, and the assumption looks exactly like a real number on the screen
VERIFIEDOf 25 projects matched to real costs off bank check images, 24 had zero cost recorded in the CRM. Total labor cost recorded across company history is $404.18. The money was spent, the checks cleared, the roofs went on. The cost simply never reached the job record.
VERIFIEDWith no cost posted, the system falls back to 65.00% estimated expenses on every contract and 10.00% payouts, which produces the same margin on every job by construction. On 58 of the 78 jobs carrying a sold value, the overhead figure is exactly 10.00% of the sale, to the penny. That is not a coincidence and it is not a measurement.
INFERENCEThis is the habit that gives the production side of the business a reason to open the software at all. It is also the one that makes every other number on this page mean something, because margin is where all of it lands.
DISCIPLINE 4Squares on every job
The fields
jobSquares, plus roofPitch and wasteFactor. None of the three exist today and they have to be created
The moment
At contract signing, off the measurement report that has already been bought
Who holds it
Whoever sells the job and orders the report
What breaks
There is no unit. Revenue, material and labor never share a denominator, so no two jobs can be compared and no price can be defended with anything except experience
VERIFIEDThere is no square count, pitch or waste factor field anywhere in the system. Meanwhile the measurement reports are already paid for: 71 charges over 24 months, $4,645.25. The number is bought, read once, used to order material, and then thrown away.
INFERENCESquares are what turn discipline 3 into pricing. Cost per job tells you whether one roof made money. Cost per square tells you what to charge on the next one, what a steep roof really costs you, and whether a bid is thin before you sign it rather than after.
INFERENCERight now production volume can only be reconstructed backwards out of material orders. That works well enough to answer a question once, but it is not something anyone can pull on a Monday morning, and it does not exist at all for a job that has not been ordered yet.
DISCIPLINE 5Contract value is what was signed
The field
contractValue, carrying the signed contract amount only
The moment
At contract signing, and again at final invoice if the scope changed and the change was agreed
Who holds it
Whoever signs the customer, and whoever issues the final invoice
What breaks
The receivable book stops being money owed and becomes a mix of money owed and money hoped for. Nobody can tell which is which by looking
VERIFIEDOf $1,582,182 of recorded receivable, $799,537 sits on a single job as an unagreed carrier estimate. That is 50.5% of the entire receivable book, on one record, and it is not money anyone owes you.
INFERENCEA carrier estimate is a negotiating position and belongs on the job as one. The contract is what a customer signed. Keeping the two in separate places is what lets you look at the receivable list and know what it means, which matters most in the week you actually need to know.

A sixty second check, not a report. Once a week, pick one job that closed that week and look at it on screen. Lead source, material cost, labor payout, squares. Four fields, one record. If all four are there, the habits are holding. If one is blank, you know which moment slipped and which role was holding the file, without anybody having to run anything.

Report or numberWhat it is actually showing you
DO NOT USE Any margin or profitability reportThe 65.00% expense and 10.00% payout defaults, arranged in a chart. Discipline 3 has to run a full season before any of these mean anything

The order these unlock in, and why. Lead source and completion status both start paying the day they are switched on, because both are a single action inside a screen someone is already looking at. Cost per job needs a season of real invoices behind it before a margin report is worth reading. Squares depend on nothing except the fields being created, but they only become pricing once cost per job is real, because a cost per square needs a cost. Contract value is independent of all of it and can be corrected on the one job that distorts the book. No dates here, because the pace is yours. The dependencies are not.
