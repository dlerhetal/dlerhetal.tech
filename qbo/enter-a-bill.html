Enter a Bill in QuickBooks Online

Recording something you owe now and will pay later, so the amount you owe is on record.
QBO Wizard  |  Written for an owner doing this alone, with no bookkeeping background

What a bill is, in plain words

A vendor has done something for you, or sold you something, and sent paper asking for money. The money has not left your bank yet. Entering it as a bill puts it on a list of what you owe and when it is due. That list is called accounts payable, which just means the total of everything you owe suppliers but have not paid yet.

If you get nothing else from this page: a bill is entered before you pay, and paid later on a different screen. The two halves are separate on purpose.

Before you start

  • What you need: a computer with a web browser. This can be done on a phone, but the bill screen is cramped and the amount boxes are easy to mis-tap, so use a real keyboard if you have one.
  • Your QuickBooks Online sign in, on a login that is allowed to enter transactions. If your login is set to view only or reports only, the button in Step 2 simply is not there. Only a company admin can change that, so if you are not one, this needs another person before you can start.
  • The actual bill in front of you, on paper or as a PDF. You need five things off it: the vendor name, the vendor's own invoice number, the date printed on it, the due date or the payment terms, and the amount.
  • If this vendor has never been used before, you will create them as you go. That is Step 4 and it takes about a minute.
  • Time: about 3 minutes once you have done two of them. Budget 10 for the first.

Read this first. Four ways this goes wrong.

  • A Bill is not an Expense and it is not a Check. Use Bill only when the money has not left the bank yet. If you already paid it, you are on the wrong screen. See Which screen do I want?
    If you already did it wrong: this is recoverable. Open the transaction, and if the title at the top says Expense or Check, delete it and re-enter it as a Bill. Nothing is lost.
  • Never pay a bill using the Expense or Check screen. That records money leaving the bank but leaves the bill sitting there marked unpaid, so the same cost is counted twice and your expenses read high forever after.
    If you already did it wrong: delete the Expense or Check you created, then pay the bill properly through Pay bills. Do this before the month is reconciled, because unpicking it afterward is much harder.
  • "Schedule online payment" is not the same as recording a payment. After you save a bill, QuickBooks offers it. It enrolls you in paying that vendor through QuickBooks, it cannot be switched off, and it fires on its own date whether the money is in the account or not. See Step 10.
    If you already did it wrong: open the bill, cancel the scheduled payment before its send date, and confirm nothing left the bank.
  • Entering the same bill twice is the single most common mistake. Always fill in the vendor's invoice number in the Bill no. field. QuickBooks watches that field and warns you when a number repeats. Leave it blank and it cannot help you. See Step 6.
    If you already did it twice: find both, keep the one that has a payment attached or the earlier one, and delete the other.

Nothing on this page is irreversible as long as you have not yet reconciled the month. Reconciling is the monthly check that your books agree with the bank statement. Once a month is reconciled, deleting things inside it breaks that check, so fix mistakes while they are fresh.

What you are entering (fill this in, it follows you down the page)

Type these once. Every later step shows your values back to you, so you are never comparing the screen against something you are holding in your head. Everything you type stays in this browser on this computer and goes nowhere else.

The steps

1Sign in to QuickBooks Online

Open a browser and go to:

https://qbo.intuit.com

Sign in. If more than one company is listed, pick the one you are working in.

Why it matters: entering transactions into the wrong company file is slow to find and slow to undo, because nothing about it looks wrong until the numbers do.
Looks right: you land on a Dashboard or Business overview page, and the company name is in the top bar.
Looks wrong: the left menu has no Expenses section. You are probably in a simplified self employed product, which has no Bills at all.
Already gone wrong: if you have been entering into the wrong company, stop before you enter any more. Each transaction has to be deleted one at a time from the wrong file and entered in the right one. It is tedious but nothing is lost.
2Open the new transaction menu

In the top left corner, click the green + New button. It sits above the left hand menu, not inside it.

A panel opens with columns for Customers, Vendors, Employees and Other. Everything you ever create in QuickBooks starts from this one button.

Why it matters: most people go hunting in the left menu for a way to create things. The left menu is for looking at lists. This button is for making new records.
Looks right: you can see a column headed Vendors.
Looks wrong: there is no + New button at all.
Already gone wrong: no + New button means your login is view only or reports only. There is no way around it from your side. A company admin has to change your user role. If you are the owner and the admin login belonged to someone who has left, you can recover the admin account through the sign in page's account recovery, but expect identity checks and allow a day for it.
3Choose Bill

Under the Vendors column, click Bill.

It sits at the top of that column, with Expense, Check and Pay bills nearby. Those three are the ones you do not want right now, and they are close enough together to catch you.

Why it matters: Expense and Check both assume the money has already left. Picking one of those means the cash goes out of your books on a day the bank says it did not, and the month will not reconcile.
Looks right: a screen opens titled Bill with a Vendor dropdown at the top left.
Already gone wrong: if the screen says Expense or Check, do not try to convert it. There is no convert. Close without saving and click Bill instead. If you already saved it, open it from Expenses, delete it, and start again.
4Pick the vendor

Click the Vendor dropdown at the top and start typing the vendor name. A vendor is simply anyone you pay: a supplier, a subcontractor, the phone company.

If they are already in the list, click the name. Their address and payment terms fill in by themselves.

If they are not in the list, click + Add new at the top of the dropdown, type the name exactly as it appears on the bill, then Save. The address can wait.

Why it matters: everything you ever want to know later, what you spend with this supplier, what you still owe them, is gathered under this one name. Two spellings means two separate answers and neither is right.
Watch out: two entries that look almost the same, for example one with "Inc" and one without. Pick the one that already has history.
Already gone wrong: if you have created a near duplicate vendor, QuickBooks can merge them. Rename the one you want to lose so that it exactly matches the name of the one you want to keep, and save. QuickBooks will offer to merge the two. Merging cannot be undone, so be sure which one you are keeping before you confirm.
5Set the dates and terms
  1. Bill date: the date printed on the vendor's invoice, not today's date.
  2. Terms: if the bill says Net 30, meaning payment is due 30 days after the bill date, pick Net 30 and the due date fills itself in. If the bill states no terms, skip this.
  3. Due date: only type this if you skipped Terms. Otherwise leave what QuickBooks worked out.
Why it matters: the bill date is what decides how old the debt looks on your reports, and the due date is what decides the order you pay people in. Using today's date on a bill that arrived three weeks ago makes an overdue account look current, and you pay it late.
Looks right: the due date is later than the bill date, and matches what the vendor expects.
Already gone wrong: dates on a bill are safely editable. Open the bill from Expenses, correct the date, and save. The only caution is a bill dated inside a month you have already reconciled, in which case change the date and then check that month still reconciles.
6Type the vendor's invoice number

In the Bill no. field, type the vendor's invoice number, exactly as printed.

Do not skip this field. It is the only thing standing between you and paying the same bill twice. When a number is already used for that vendor, QuickBooks shows a warning right under the field.

Why it matters: with no bookkeeper checking behind you, this warning is your entire duplicate defense. It costs four seconds to type.
If you see a warning that the bill number has already been used: stop. Do not alter the number to make the warning go away. Close without saving and look at the existing bill, because it is probably this same one arriving a second time.
Already gone wrong: to find duplicates entered earlier, go to Expenses, filter the Type to Bill, and sort by amount. Identical amounts to the same vendor sitting next to each other are the ones to look at. Delete the copy that has no payment attached. If you have already paid both, that is a call to the vendor for a refund or a credit, not something QuickBooks can fix.
7Category details, or Item details?

Partway down there are two sections that look almost the same. This is the part that confuses everybody, so here is the plain version.

SectionUse it when
Category details Almost always. A category is just the name of the spending bucket the cost belongs in: postage, utilities, computer services, supplies, professional fees.
Item details Only when you are buying goods you have set up as products to resell. If products have never been set up in your file, this section will be empty and you can ignore it entirely.

Unless you know you are buying stock to resell, use Category details.

Why it matters: the two sections post the cost to different places. Using Item details on an ordinary overhead bill puts the cost somewhere your profit report does not expect, and the profit number comes out wrong.
8Fill in the line

On the first row under Category details:

  1. Category: start typing the spending bucket, for example Postage and Delivery or Computer Services. Pick the closest one that already exists. Do not create a new one just because nothing is a perfect fit. The full list of these buckets is called the chart of accounts, and a list that grows a new line every week stops telling you anything.
  2. Description: what it was for, in plain words.
  3. Amount: the amount.

If the bill covers several different things, use a second row for each. The rows have to add up to the total on the paper.

Why it matters: the category you pick here is the only thing that decides where this cost shows up on your profit report. Put fuel under office supplies for a year and you will genuinely not know what fuel costs you.
Looks right: the Total at the bottom right matches the total printed on the vendor's bill, to the penny.
Already gone wrong: a wrong category is the easiest thing on this page to fix. Open the bill, change the category, save. If you have used the wrong category on dozens of past bills, do not re-open them one at a time. Go to Expenses, filter to that vendor, and correct them in a batch, or accept the history and just be right from today forward.
9Attach the bill itself

Near the bottom of the screen there is an Attachments box. Drag the PDF or a photo of the bill onto it, or click it and browse.

Why it matters: you are the only person who knows what this was. When someone questions this bill in eighteen months, the attachment answers it in four seconds instead of forty minutes in a filing cabinet. It also means a lost paper copy does not matter.
10Save it

Bottom right, click the arrow next to Save and new and pick what you want:

  • Save and new if you have another bill to enter.
  • Save and close if that was the last one.
It worked if: the screen clears or closes with no error, and the bar at the bottom shows the total you expected.

Do not press "Schedule online payment"

After you save, QuickBooks puts a Schedule online payment button in front of you. It used to read "Make payment" and it no longer does the same thing. That button signs you up to pay the vendor through QuickBooks as an online payment service. It is not a way of recording that you already paid, and there is no setting that turns it off. [vendor: Intuit support states the button replaced "Make payment" and cannot be disabled]

Three reasons to leave it alone:

  • If you pay by check, it is the wrong door. Record the check through Pay bills instead, which is covered further down this page.
  • A scheduled payment fires whether the money is there or not. You want to decide what to pay after you have looked at the balance, not two weeks ahead of it.
  • It is how a bill gets paid twice. Schedule it here, write a check as well, and the books show double what actually left the bank.

To record that a bill is settled without paying through QuickBooks, use Mark as paid, or go through Pay bills. Both leave the money movement in your hands.

Already gone wrong: if the browser closed or the connection dropped mid entry, check Expenses before you re-type anything. QuickBooks saves more often than you would expect, and re-entering a bill that did save is how a duplicate is born.
11Check that it landed

Do this the first several times, until you trust it. Two minutes now, rather than a surprise at month end.

  1. In the left menu, click Expenses, then the Vendors tab.
  2. Find the vendor in the list.
  3. Their Open balance, meaning what you still owe them, should have gone up by the amount of this bill.
Why it matters: nobody is going to review this behind you. Checking the balance moved is the whole quality control department now, and it takes a minute.
Correct: the open balance went up, and the bill shows with a status of Open.
The balance did not move: most likely it saved as an Expense instead of a Bill, which happens if the wrong item was clicked back in Step 3. Open the transaction and read the title at the top of the screen. If it says Expense, delete it and start again from Step 2. The second most likely cause is that it saved against a similarly named vendor, so check the other spelling before you re-enter anything.

When it comes time to pay it

Do not use Expense and do not use Check. Both record money leaving while leaving the bill open, so the cost lands in your books twice.

+ New → Vendors column → Pay bills, tick the bill, pick the bank account the money is coming from, and save. The bill flips from Open to Paid and what you owe comes down.

That screen is also the closest thing you have to a payment plan. It lists every bill with its due date, so you can decide who gets paid this week with the facts in front of you rather than from the pile on the desk.
