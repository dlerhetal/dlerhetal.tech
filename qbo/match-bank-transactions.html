Match Downloaded Bank and Credit Card Transactions

Clearing the review queue without creating a single duplicate. Match before you Add.
QBO Wizard  |  Written for an owner doing this alone, with no bookkeeping background

What this queue is, in plain words

When your bank or credit card is connected to QuickBooks, every transaction the bank processes gets downloaded into a waiting list called For review. Nothing in that list is in your books yet. It is a pile of raw bank lines waiting for you to say what each one is.

You do one of two things with each line. If the transaction is already in QuickBooks because you entered it, you Match it, which just links the two together. If it is not in QuickBooks at all, you Add it, which creates it. Getting those two the wrong way round is how the same cost ends up in your books twice, and it is the most common damage done in QuickBooks by someone new to it.

Before you start

  • What you need: a computer. This screen is a wide table with a lot of small controls and it is genuinely painful on a phone.
  • The bank or card already connected to QuickBooks. Connecting an account is a different job from this one. It needs the online banking login, and it usually triggers a security code sent by text, which often means a phone that may not be in your hand. If the account is not connected yet, line that up before you sit down.
  • A QuickBooks login allowed to enter transactions. A reports only login cannot do any of this, and only a company admin can change it.
  • Some idea of what the business actually bought. This job is twenty percent clicking and eighty percent knowing what a payee is. Where you do not recognize a name, park it rather than guessing.
  • Time: 10 to 15 minutes a week if you keep up. Six months of backlog is a full day and the accuracy is worse, which is the whole argument for doing it weekly.

Read this first. This screen makes duplicates faster than any other.

  • Add is not Match. Add creates a brand new transaction. Match ties the bank line to one that already exists. If you already entered the bill, the check or the invoice, clicking Add records the cost a second time. Always look for a match first. See Step 5.
    If you already did it: fixable. On the Categorized tab there is an Undo on each row. Undo sends the bank line back to For review and removes what it created, then you can match it properly. Do this before you reconcile the month.
  • Never Add money that came in from a customer with an open invoice. It has to be matched to that invoice, or recorded as a payment against it. Adding it straight to income leaves the invoice sitting unpaid forever and counts the sale twice. See Step 7.
  • Money moved between two of your own accounts is a transfer, not income and not an expense. It appears twice, once leaving one account and once arriving in the other. Recorded wrong it inflates both your sales and your costs, which affects what you owe in tax. See Step 8.
  • Exclude is not a filing cabinet. Excluding hides a line and records nothing. Used on something you simply did not understand, it silently removes a real transaction from the books, and it will not be there at reconciling time.
    If you already did it: the Excluded tab still holds it, and you can send it back to For review. Nothing is lost as long as you remember it is there.
  • Do not let a rule run on autopilot until you trust it. A rule set to add automatically posts transactions without you ever seeing them.

Add, Match, Transfer or Exclude?

ButtonUse it whenWhat it does
Match The transaction is already in QuickBooks: a bill you paid, a payment on an invoice, a check you wrote. Links the two. Nothing new is created. This is the safe default.
Add (some layouts say Categorize or Confirm) Nothing in the books represents this yet. A card swipe nobody entered. Creates a new expense or deposit using the category shown.
Record as transfer Money moved between two accounts that both belong to the business. Records one movement instead of a fake expense and a fake sale.
Exclude Only a genuine double download, or activity from before the date you started keeping books in QuickBooks. Takes it off the list and records nothing. Use rarely.

The steps

1Sign in to QuickBooks Online

Open a browser and go to:

https://qbo.intuit.com
2Open the downloaded transactions

In the left menu, click Transactions, then Bank transactions.

Unconfirmed Older layouts call this Banking in the left menu, with a Banking tab inside it. Same screen, older name. If you see no Transactions entry, look for Banking.

Looks right: a row of account tiles across the top, each showing a bank balance, a QuickBooks balance and a count of items waiting.
The page invites you to connect an account and shows no tiles: nothing is connected. That is a separate setup job and it needs your online banking login, so it may not be something you can finish in the next five minutes. In the meantime you can still enter transactions by hand, and you can still reconcile from the paper statement.
3Pick the account and the For review tab

Click the tile for the account you are clearing, then make sure you are on the For review tab. The other two tabs are Categorized, meaning already dealt with, and Excluded.

The count on that tab is how many items are waiting. If the number is large, work oldest first.

Why oldest first: matching works by looking for a transaction you already entered. Working forward in time means the older entries are still available to match against, instead of being buried under newer ones.
4Read a row before you touch it

Each row shows the date, the description the bank sent, and the amount. QuickBooks adds its own guess at who the payee is and which category it belongs in, and a note when it thinks it has found a match.

Click anywhere on the row to expand it. Expanding changes nothing, and it is the only way to see what QuickBooks is actually proposing to do.

The guessed category is a guess. It is based on the bank's description text and on what similar transactions were called before, sometimes by a previous bookkeeper who was also guessing. It is right often enough to be dangerous.
5Look for a match first, always

If the row offers a match, it says so, naming the transaction it found and its date.

  1. Check the amount is identical.
  2. Check the date is close. A check can clear a week or two after you wrote it, so a gap is normal. A gap of months usually means it is the wrong match.
  3. Check the name is the right one.
  4. If all three hold, click Match.

If several candidates are offered, open the list and pick deliberately. If none is right, there is usually a link to search for other possible matches by date and amount.

Why it matters: a match records nothing new, it just connects the bank line to the entry you already made. That is what keeps one real payment from becoming two recorded ones.
Correct: the row leaves For review and shows on the Categorized tab as matched. No new transaction was created.
You clicked Add on something already entered: go to the Categorized tab, find that row, and click Undo. That removes what it created and returns the line to For review, where you can match it properly. If the month has already been reconciled, undoing will unbalance it, so in that case leave it and instead delete the duplicate transaction, then re-reconcile that month.
6If there is genuinely nothing to match, categorize it
  1. Vendor or Customer: pick the real name, not the garbled description the bank sent. Consistent names are what make your reports readable later.
  2. Category: the spending bucket it belongs in. Pick the closest one that already exists. Do not invent a new one for a perfect fit.
  3. Add a short memo if the payee name will mean nothing to you in six months.
  4. Click Add (some layouts say Confirm).
Why it matters: the category decides which line of your profit report this shows up on. That report is how you will decide what to cut, so a sloppy category today is a bad decision later.
You do not know what something is: leave it in the queue. Build a short list of unknowns and settle them in one pass, with the card statement or a phone call. An honest blank is worth more than a confident wrong answer, because a wrong answer never gets revisited.
7Handle money coming in carefully

A deposit is the single easiest thing to get wrong on this screen, and the mistakes cost real money, because they change what you appear to have earned.

What the deposit isWhat to do
Payment on an invoice you sent Match it. If the payment was never recorded in QuickBooks, record it first (+ New → Customers → Receive payment), then match.
One lump deposit covering several customer checks QuickBooks can match one bank line to several recorded payments at once. Tick all of them so the total agrees.
A customer paying on the spot with no invoice A sales receipt is the clean way. Categorizing straight to income also works if you do not need a record against that customer.
You putting your own money in, or a loan arriving Neither of these is income, and treating them as income means paying tax on your own money. This one is worth a five minute call to an accountant rather than a guess.
Already added a customer payment straight to income: undo it on the Categorized tab, then record it properly against the invoice. If you leave it, you will see the invoice still showing as unpaid while the money is clearly in the bank, and you will chase a customer who already paid you.
8Money moved between your own accounts

A card payment out of checking, or a move to savings, is a transfer: the same money leaving one of your accounts and arriving in another. It is not an expense and the arriving side is not a sale.

  1. Expand the row.
  2. Choose the transfer option, usually a tab or a radio labeled Record as transfer.
  3. Pick the other account, the one the money went to or came from.
  4. Save.

When the other account is also connected, the matching side shows up in that account's queue. Match it there rather than recording a second transfer, or the money appears to move twice.

Why it matters: recorded as an expense and a sale, a single sweep between your own accounts inflates both your revenue and your costs. Do it monthly and your business looks bigger and less profitable than it is, to you and to anyone you show the numbers to.
9Use rules, carefully, for the boring ones

A rule tells QuickBooks that anything matching a description should be filled in a set way. When the same payee arrives every month for the same thing, this saves real time. Rules live on the bank transactions page, usually on a Rules tab.

Keep them to things that never change: the phone bill, the rent, the payroll fee. Leave the automatic option off at first, so the rule fills in the detail and you still press the button yourself. Turn it on only after you have watched the rule behave for a month.

A careless rule is worse than no rule. One that matches on a common word will quietly miscategorize everything containing it, and because it runs automatically you never see it happen.
A rule has been miscategorizing for months: turn the rule off first so it stops, then fix the rule, then correct the past transactions. The Categorized tab lets you select several rows at once, which is much faster than opening them one by one.
10Finish, then prove it

When For review is empty, or everything left is genuinely unknown and parked on purpose, the sorting is done. Sorting is not the same as being right, so prove it:

Reconcile the account against the statement. That is the step that proves the books agree with the bank. Everything before it is preparation.

Done well: For review is at or near zero, the Categorized tab reads sensibly with real names on it, and the reconciliation comes out at a difference of 0.00.
