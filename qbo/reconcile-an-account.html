Reconcile an Account in QuickBooks Online

Proving the books agree with the statement, and what to do when the difference will not go to zero.
QBO Wizard  |  Written for an owner doing this alone, with no bookkeeping background

What reconciling is, in plain words

Once a month the bank sends a statement: a list of every dollar in and out, and the balance at the end. Reconciling means going down that list and confirming that every line on it is also in QuickBooks, and that nothing extra is in QuickBooks that the bank never saw. When the two agree to the penny, the account is reconciled.

That is the only thing in bookkeeping that actually proves your numbers. Everything else is typing. This is checking. Done monthly it takes fifteen minutes. Skipped for six months it becomes the reason you cannot trust a single report in the file.

Before you start

  • What you need: a computer with a real screen. Reconciling on a phone is technically possible and practically miserable, because the screen is a wide table.
  • The statement itself, as a PDF or on paper, for one single month. You need two numbers off it: the statement ending date and the statement ending balance, which is the balance printed at the bottom of that statement. Everything here hangs on those two numbers.
  • A QuickBooks login that is allowed to reconcile. A limited user, and any reports only user, will not see the tool at all. Only a company admin can widen that, so if you are not one, this needs another person before you start.
  • Every transaction for that month already in the books. If your bank feeds into QuickBooks automatically, work that queue to empty first. That is a separate job with its own wizard: Match downloaded bank and credit card transactions.
  • The previous month already reconciled. Do these in order, oldest first. Skipping a month and coming back is the main reason beginning balances go wrong, and a wrong beginning balance stops you dead.
  • Time: 10 to 20 minutes for a clean month. The first month of an account nobody has reconciled in a year can take most of a morning. That is normal and it only happens once.

Read this first. Four ways this goes wrong.

  • Never force it to balance with an adjustment. If you click finish while the difference is not zero, QuickBooks offers to write the gap off as an adjusting entry. That buries a real error in an expense bucket where you will never find it again, and every report from then on carries it. See Step 9.
    If you already took one: it is recoverable but unpleasant. Undo that reconciliation, delete the adjusting entry (look for a transaction named something like Reconciliation Discrepancy on the date the month ended), and reconcile again properly.
  • Never change the beginning balance to make it match. A wrong beginning balance means something inside an already reconciled month was edited or deleted afterward. Fix that, do not paper over it. See Step 5.
  • Undoing a finished reconciliation is not a small thing. It strips the reconciled mark from every transaction in that month at once, and on most plans only an accountant login can put it back efficiently. It is reversible in principle and a bad afternoon in practice.
  • Do not tick a line just because it is in the list. You tick a line because you found it on the statement. Ticking to close a gap is the same mistake as taking the adjustment, just slower and harder to find later.

The steps

1Sign in to QuickBooks Online

Open a browser and go to:

https://qbo.intuit.com

Sign in, and check the company name in the top bar before you touch anything.

Why it matters: if you have more than one company file, reconciling the right statement against the wrong file wastes an hour and teaches you nothing.
2Empty the review queue first

If the account is connected to your bank, QuickBooks downloads transactions into a waiting list called For review. Clear that list before you start here.

Full walk through: Match downloaded bank and credit card transactions.

Why it matters: anything still sitting in review is not in the books yet. It cannot be ticked off, so it turns into a difference you will spend twenty minutes hunting for a cause you already knew about.
Ready: the For review count for this account is zero, or everything left is dated after the statement ending date.
Already gone wrong: if you are already mid reconciliation and realize the queue is full, do not abandon what you have ticked. Save the reconciliation and come back. QuickBooks keeps your ticks.
3Open the Reconcile tool

There are two places this lives, depending on your QuickBooks Online version and how recently the menus were updated. Try them in this order:

  1. The gear icon in the top right, then under the Tools column, click Reconcile.
  2. If it is not there, look in the left menu under Transactions for a Reconcile entry.

Unconfirmed Intuit has moved this item between the gear menu and the left hand Transactions menu more than once, and an accountant style layout differs again. Both paths reach the same screen, so use whichever one your file shows.

Looks right: a page headed Reconcile with an account dropdown and two empty boxes for an ending balance and an ending date.
There is no Reconcile entry anywhere: your login does not include it. Nothing you can do from your side changes that. A company admin has to widen your access. If the admin login belonged to someone who has left the business, you can recover it through the account recovery on the sign in page, but expect identity checks and allow a day.
4Pick the account

Choose the account from the Account dropdown at the top.

Bank accounts and credit cards both reconcile here and the process is identical. The only difference is that on a credit card the balance is money you owe, so it reads the other way round.

Why it matters: credit cards are the accounts most often left unreconciled, and they are where quiet recurring charges hide. Reconcile them too.
If you started on the wrong account: nothing is saved until you finish, so back out and start again. If you already finished a month against the wrong account, undo that reconciliation before doing anything else.
5Check the beginning balance before anything else

QuickBooks fills in a Beginning balance by itself. It is the ending balance from the last month you reconciled. You cannot edit it on this screen, and that is deliberate.

Compare it to the beginning balance printed on the statement.

Why it matters: if the starting point is wrong, everything built on top of it is wrong. Carrying on regardless just moves the problem into next month, where it is harder to find.
They match: carry on to Step 6.
They do not match. What to do:
  1. This always means the same thing: a transaction inside a month you already reconciled was later changed, deleted, or had its amount edited.
  2. Go to Reports and search for the reconciliation discrepancy report for this account. It lists exactly which transactions were touched and by how much.
  3. Put those transactions back the way they were. Usually the amount or the date was edited and you can simply set it back.
  4. If the report is empty or the difference still will not resolve, stop here and get an accountant on it for an hour. This is the one problem on this page that is genuinely worth paying someone to solve, because the alternative is that no month after it can ever be trusted.
6Type the ending date and ending balance
  1. Ending date: the statement ending date, exactly. Not the end of the calendar month, unless the statement says so. Plenty of credit cards close on the 17th.
  2. Ending balance: the statement ending balance, to the penny, straight off the statement. Do not round it and do not adjust it to what you think it should be.

Then click the button that starts it, usually labeled something like Start reconciling in the lower right.

Why it matters: these two numbers are the bank's version of the truth. Your job for the next fifteen minutes is to make your books agree with them, so typing them wrong sends you hunting for an error that does not exist.
Already gone wrong: if the difference is a strange number and nothing you tick moves it sensibly, come back here and read both numbers off the statement again before you spend another minute. A mistyped ending balance is the most common cause of an unexplainable difference, and there is no shame in it.
7Understand the screen you just landed on

Across the top is a summary strip. The number that matters is the last one, usually labeled Difference. Your entire job is to get that to 0.00.

What you seeWhat it means
Statement ending balanceWhat you typed in Step 6. The bank's version.
Cleared balanceYour beginning balance plus everything you have ticked so far. Cleared just means the bank has actually processed it.
DifferenceThe gap between those two. Must reach 0.00.

Below that, the transactions are usually split into tabs for money out and money in, with an option to see everything in one list. Work one tab at a time. It is far easier to keep your place.

8Tick off what is on the statement

Go down the statement, line by line, and find each line in QuickBooks. Not the other way round. The statement is the master list.

  1. Find the matching line by date and amount.
  2. Click its tick box on the right to mark it cleared.
  3. Move to the next line on the statement.

Sorting the QuickBooks list by amount rather than by date makes this much faster, because you are hunting for a specific dollar figure, not a date.

Why it matters: working from the statement guarantees you notice anything the bank did that your books do not know about, including fees, and including a payment that went out that you did not authorize.
Going well: the Difference figure shrinks toward zero as you work.
A statement line is nowhere in QuickBooks: the transaction was never entered. Leave this reconciliation open, open a second browser tab, enter it properly there as whatever it actually is (a bill payment, an expense, a deposit, a bank fee), then come back to this tab and refresh. It will appear in the list, ready to tick.
A QuickBooks line is nowhere on the statement: leave it unticked. That is normal for a check written near month end that has not been cashed yet. If it is still unticked three months later, it is a check that was lost or never cashed, and that is worth a phone call.
9When the difference will not go to zero

This happens to everyone, every so often, including people who do it for a living. Work this list in order. It is roughly the order of likelihood.

CheckWhat it catches
Divide the difference by 9. Is it a whole number? Two digits typed the wrong way round, 54.18 entered as 45.18. Very common.
Is the difference exactly equal to one line on the statement? Something you have not ticked, or never entered at all.
Is the difference exactly twice a transaction? The same thing entered twice, or entered with the sign the wrong way round.
Read the ending balance off the statement again. A mistyped ending balance. It is wrong more often than people admit.
Is the ending date right? A wrong date drags in part of the next month.
Any duplicates in the list? Same payee, same amount, a day apart, usually one typed by hand and one from the bank feed.
Do not take the adjustment. Finishing while the difference is not zero lets QuickBooks write the gap off for you. That closes the month with a known error inside it, and the error stays in the file permanently.
If you genuinely cannot find it: save and close the reconciliation, which keeps every tick you have made, and come back to it with fresh eyes or with an hour of an accountant's time. An unfinished reconciliation costs you nothing. A forced one costs you the ability to trust the account, which is the whole reason you are doing this.
10Finish, and keep the report

With the difference at 0.00, click the finish button in the top right, usually labeled something like Finish now.

QuickBooks shows the reconciliation report. Save it as a PDF and file it next to the statement PDF, every month, in the same folder.

Why it matters: when a lender, a buyer or the tax authority asks what happened in a month two years ago, this report plus the statement is the complete answer, and you will not have to reconstruct anything.
Done: a success message and the report on screen for the account through the ending date.
11Verify it stuck
  1. Open the account register, which is just the running list of everything in that account: left menu Transactions, then Chart of accounts (the list of all your accounts and spending buckets), then the View register link on that account.
  2. Look at the narrow column near the right edge. Reconciled lines show R. Lines the bank has processed but that are not reconciled show C. Neither shows blank.
Correct: everything you ticked, dated on or before the ending date, now shows R.
Never toggle that R by hand from inside the register. It is clickable and it should not be. Changing it silently breaks next month's beginning balance and you will not know why.
If you have already clicked one by accident: click it back to R immediately, then reconcile next month as normal. If the beginning balance next month is off by the amount of that transaction, that is what happened, and setting it back to R fixes it.
