Create and Send an Invoice in QuickBooks Online

Billing a customer who will pay later, and sending it so you can see when it was opened.
QBO Wizard  |  Written for an owner doing this alone, with no bookkeeping background

What an invoice is, in plain words

An invoice is a bill you send to a customer for work you have already done, which they will pay later. Creating one puts the amount on a list of money owed to you, called accounts receivable. That list is what you chase.

If the customer paid you at the time, in cash or on a card, that is not an invoice, it is a sales receipt. Using an invoice for money you already have creates a debt that does not exist and it will sit on your reports until you notice.

Before you start

  • What you need: a computer is easiest. The mobile app can create an invoice and it is fine for a one line job billed from the truck, but the lines and the terms are fiddly on a small screen.
  • A QuickBooks login allowed to create sales. A reports only login cannot, and only a company admin can widen it.
  • The customer's email address. Without it you can print or download a PDF, but you cannot send from QuickBooks, and you lose the record of when it was sent and when it was opened.
  • What you are billing, in writing. The work order, the signed estimate, the timesheet. Invoicing from memory is where disputes start, and a dispute is an unpaid invoice.
  • The terms you agreed. Due on receipt, Net 15, Net 30. "Net 30" simply means payment is due 30 days from the invoice date. If nothing was agreed, decide now rather than after.
  • Sales tax: if what you sell is taxable, sales tax has to be set up in the company already. Setting it up in the middle of your first invoice is a bad time to learn it, and getting it wrong is a filing problem rather than a typo. If you are unsure, that is one accountant question worth asking once.
  • Time: about 5 minutes. Less once the customer and the line items exist.

Read this first. Four expensive habits.

  • Do not invoice work that was already paid for on the spot. That is a Sales receipt. An invoice creates a debt that was never owed. See Which screen do I want?
    If you already did it: delete the invoice if no payment has been attached, then create a sales receipt instead.
  • Do not mark an invoice paid to make it disappear from the list. That tells your books money arrived when it did not, and the month will not reconcile against the bank.
  • Do not edit an invoice after a payment has been applied to it. Changing the amount underneath an applied payment leaves a stray credit or a stray balance, and untangling it takes far longer than creating it did. Issue a credit memo instead, which is a document that reduces what a customer owes without destroying the original invoice. See Step 10.
  • Do not send it from your own email program. Sent from QuickBooks you get a record of when it went, when it was opened, and a pay link if card payments are turned on. Sent from your inbox, QuickBooks still shows it as never sent, and in three weeks you will not know which customers were billed.

Nothing here is irreversible while the invoice is unpaid and the month is unreconciled. Once a payment is applied, or the month is closed, fixes become credit memos rather than edits.

The steps

1Sign in to QuickBooks Online

Open a browser and go to:

https://qbo.intuit.com
2Open a new invoice

Click the green + New button in the top left, then under the Customers column click Invoice.

There is a second route: left menu Sales, then the Invoices tab, then the new invoice button on the right. Same screen.

Unconfirmed Intuit has rolled out more than one invoice layout. On the newer one the fields sit in a single column with a live preview beside them; on the older one they run across the top in a band. The fields are the same in both, so this guide names fields, not positions on the screen.

No + New button: your login is view or reports only. A company admin has to change your user role. If the admin login belonged to someone who has left, recover it through the account recovery on the sign in page and allow a day for the identity checks.
3Pick the customer

In the Customer field, start typing the customer name.

Already there: click the name and the address, terms and email fill in themselves.

Not there: click + Add new, type the name, save. The address can wait, but add the email now, because you need it in Step 9.

Why it matters: everything you will ever want to know about this customer, what they owe, how slowly they pay, how much they are worth to you, gathers under this one name. Two spellings means two half answers.
You have created a near duplicate customer: QuickBooks can merge them. Rename the one you want to lose so it exactly matches the name of the one you are keeping, and save. It will offer to merge. Merging cannot be undone, so be certain which record you are keeping first.
4Check the email address

The Email field near the top should show the customer email. Correct it here if it is wrong, and then fix it on the customer record too, so you are not doing this again next month.

To send to more than one person, separate addresses with a comma. Most layouts also offer Cc and Bcc.

Why it matters: "we never received it" is the most common reason an invoice sits unpaid, and it is often true. Sending to the person who actually processes payments, rather than to the person who hired you, is worth a week of waiting.
5Set the date, the terms and the invoice number
  1. Invoice date: the date you are billing. This starts the clock, so do not backdate it to make a month look better.
  2. Terms: what you agreed. The Due date fills in from it.
  3. Invoice no.: QuickBooks proposes the next number, which is usually what you want. Only override it if you run your own numbering, and never reuse a number.
Why it matters: the invoice date decides how old the debt looks when you come to chase it, and the due date decides when you are entitled to start chasing. Without those two right, the report that tells you who is late is not telling you anything.
Looks right: the due date is later than the invoice date and matches what you agreed.
6Fill in the lines

Each row of the table is one thing you are billing for.

  1. Product or service: pick the item. This is what decides which income line the money lands on, which is why it matters more than it looks.
  2. Description: plain words the customer will recognize. This is the part they actually read, and a vague description is the most common reason an invoice sits in somebody's pile while they work out what it was for.
  3. Qty and Rate: the amount calculates itself.
  4. Add a row for each separate item. Detail reduces arguments.

If the item you need does not exist, you can add it from the dropdown. Keep that list short and meaningful. Forty near identical items makes your sales reports useless to you.

Looks right: the total at the bottom equals the total you expected, including sales tax if it applies.
Wrong amount, already sent: if no payment has been applied, open the invoice, correct it, and send it again. If a payment has been applied, do not edit it. Issue a credit memo for the difference (+ New → Customers → Credit memo) and apply it, which leaves a clean trail of what happened.
7Message, attachments and payment options
  • Message on invoice: what the customer sees. Payment instructions go here.
  • Message on statement: only appears if you send statements. Usually leave it alone.
  • Attachments: the signed work order, the photos, the delivery ticket. These can be sent with the invoice, and they settle most disputes before they start.
  • If online payments are turned on for the company, the options to accept card or bank transfer appear on this screen. That puts a pay button in the customer's email and visibly speeds up collection, at the cost of a processing fee. That is your decision to make, not a data entry one.
Why it matters: with nobody else answering the phone, every dispute you prevent is an afternoon you keep. An attached work order prevents most of them.
8Preview it before it leaves

Use the print or preview option, usually along the bottom bar or behind a print icon, and look at the invoice the way the customer will see it.

  • Is the customer name spelled right?
  • Does the description explain the charge to someone who was not there?
  • Is the total what you expected?
  • Is your own phone number and address on it?
Why it matters: thirty seconds here replaces a phone call later, and the phone call is now yours to take.
9Send it

At the bottom right, use the save and send option. On newer layouts this is a button labeled something like Review and send, which shows the outgoing email first; on older ones it is Save and send.

The other choices on that button do what they say: save and close, save and make another, or save and share a link.

Sent: a confirmation appears and the invoice status reads Sent. It changes to Viewed when the customer opens it, which is worth knowing when they later say it never arrived.
The send option will not work: there is no email address on file. Go back to Step 4. You can still print or download the PDF and send it yourself, but then QuickBooks will not know it was sent, so note the date in the invoice memo so you can still tell later.
10Check it landed, and know what comes next
  1. Left menu Sales, then the Invoices tab.
  2. Find the invoice number. The status should read Sent, then later Viewed, then Paid.
  3. Open the customer from Sales → Customers. Their open balance should have gone up by the invoice total.

When they pay: + New → Customers → Receive payment, pick the customer, tick this invoice, choose where the money landed, save. If your bank feeds into QuickBooks, the deposit will then offer to match. Match it. Do not record the same money twice.

When they do not pay: the invoice ages, and working that list is its own job: Clear out aged accounts receivable.
