Update the 13-Week Cash Flow Model

The Monday routine. Roll the week forward, put in the real bank balance, and see the next thirteen Saturdays before they arrive.
QBO Wizard  |  Written for an owner doing this alone, with no bookkeeping background

What this workbook is, in plain words

The Cash Flow Model is an Excel workbook that lays out the next 13 weeks side by side, one column per week, each week ending on a Saturday. Every column starts with the cash you expect to have, adds the money you expect to come in, takes away the money you expect to go out, and ends with the cash left over. That ending number becomes the next week's starting number, all the way across.

It is only as good as two things: the starting cash in the first week, which must be the real bank balance, and the dates you put on the money coming in and going out. Once a week you replace last week's guesses with what actually happened, and the whole quarter redraws itself. That is this page.

Color tells you where you are allowed to type. Yellow cells are yours. The one orange cell is the starting cash, the single number the other thirteen weeks stand on. Everything else is a formula and is locked on purpose.

Before you start

  • A Windows computer with the desktop version of Excel. The workbook runs on its own buttons, and those buttons do not work in Excel in a browser or on a phone. This one is laptop or desktop only.
  • Last week's workbook, the most recent saved copy. You will make this week's copy from it in Step 2.
  • Your online banking login, for every business bank account. Checking and any savings or money market account. Banks often send a security code by text when you sign in, which means your phone needs to be in reach.
  • QuickBooks, signed in, so you can see which invoices are still open and which bills are waiting to be paid.
  • Any promises made this week: a customer who said they would pay on a certain day, a supplier who agreed to a new date. These live in your head or your email, not in the workbook.
  • Time: 20 to 30 minutes if you did it last Monday. Longer the first few times, and longer if a week was skipped.

Read this first. One button in this routine cannot be undone.

  • New Week cannot be undone. The button moves every week in the workbook over by one and there is no Undo afterward. That is why Step 2 makes a dated copy before anything else. Last week's file, left untouched, is your only way back. See Step 2.
  • Press New Week once, and only once, per Monday. Pressing it a second time rolls a second week and the workbook itself warns that forecast dates you entered are lost. If you think you pressed it twice, close without saving and start again from the copy. See Step 4.
  • The success message is not proof. The workbook shows "moved up by 1 week" even when part of it refused to move. Always check that the first date really changed. See Step 4.
  • Never add sheets, rows, notes or comments to the workbook. Use the empty yellow rows that are already there. Anything you want to remember goes in the note boxes on this page, not in the file.
  • A big one-off payment goes in whole, in the one week it will actually be paid. Never spread it across several weeks to make the chart look smoother. The bank will take it all at once, so the model must show it all at once. See Step 8.
  • Type only in yellow cells and the orange starting-cash cell. If Excel says a cell is on a protected sheet, you are in the wrong cell. Click OK and look for the yellow one. Nothing is broken.

The steps

1Open last week's workbook
  1. Open the folder where you keep the Cash Flow Model and double-click the most recent one. The date at the end of the file name tells you which is newest.
  2. If a yellow bar across the top says PROTECTED VIEW, click Enable Editing.
  3. If a bar says macros have been disabled, click Enable Content. The workbook's buttons are macros, so without this nothing in Steps 4 onward will work.
Looks right: a tab along the top of Excel named *** CFM : Cash Forecast Model ***. Click it and you see the workbook's own buttons, including Current Rolling Quarter and New Week.
A red bar says macros are blocked because the file came from the internet or another computer: close the workbook. In File Explorer, right-click the file, choose Properties, tick Unblock near the bottom of the General tab, click OK, and open it again. Excel sometimes does this to a file that arrived by email or download.
2Save this week's copy first, before touching anything

The file is named for the Saturday that ends its first week, written as year, month, day with two digits each. Once you roll forward in Step 4, the first week will end this coming Saturday, so this week's copy takes this Saturday's date.

This coming Saturday is (works out from today's date), so the date part of the new name is YYMMDD.

  1. Click File → Save As.
  2. Keep the same folder.
  3. In the name, change only the six-digit date at the end to the one above. Everything before it stays exactly as it was.
  4. Leave Save as type alone. It should already say Excel Binary Workbook.
  5. Click Save.
Why first: New Week in Step 4 cannot be undone. Excel is now working in the new copy, and last week's file is closed and untouched. If anything goes wrong this morning, you delete the new copy and start again from last week's.
Correct: the title bar at the top of Excel now shows the new name with this Saturday's date.
3Check the week that just ended against the bank

Do this before you roll forward. Right now the first column of weeks is still the week that just ended, and it shows what you expected to happen in it. In a few minutes that column rolls off the front of the model, so this is the one chance to compare.

  1. Click Current Rolling Quarter on the workbook's tab. This is the main sheet with the 13 week columns.
  2. Look down the first week column. Under (B) CASH RECEIPTS is the money you expected in. Under (C) CASH DISBURSEMENTS is the money you expected out.
  3. Sign in to online banking and look at last week's activity for every business account.
  4. For each line the model expected, decide: did it happen, yes or no? A customer payment that did not arrive, or a bill you did not pay after all, has slipped. List every slipped item in the box below, with its amount and the week you now expect it.
  5. Now put this morning's balance for each account in the boxes below. The page adds them up.
Starting cash this week, all accounts added together enter the balances below

Then find what the model expected you would have. On the same sheet, the row (E.1) ENDING CASH AVAILABLE near the bottom, first week column. Type it here.

Difference: enter both numbers above

Example with made-up numbers: checking 12,500 plus savings 2,500 is 15,000 in the bank. The model expected 14,000. The bank is 1,000 higher than expected. The usual reason is a bill you expected to pay last week that has not gone out yet, which means it is still coming and belongs on your slipped list.

Why it matters: a gap you can explain is fine. A gap you cannot explain means something is moving money that the model does not know about, and every week after this one is off by at least that much.
A check you already mailed has not cleared the bank yet: do not subtract it from the balance. Leave the bank number exactly as the bank shows it, and put the check on your slipped list so it goes back in as a payment this week.
The first week column is two Saturdays old because a Monday was missed: stop here and note it in the box below. Do not press New Week twice in one sitting to catch up; that is the one move the workbook itself warns loses data.
4Roll the week forward with New Week

This moves every week over by one. The week that just ended drops off the front, this week becomes the first column, and a new thirteenth week appears at the end. You never type the dates yourself; the button does it.

  1. Make sure you are on the main sheet: click Current Rolling Quarter.
  2. Click New Week (the lightning bolt) on the workbook's tab.
  3. A box titled Continue? asks: "Do you REALLY want to ADVANCE to the next week - this is NoT reversible..." You made the copy in Step 2, so click Yes.
  4. Wait. It takes a moment. A box titled Forecast Model Advanced says "All data in the Forecast Model has now been moved up by 1 week". Click OK.
  5. Now check it. Look at the first date across the top of the week columns. It must be this coming Saturday, this Saturday.
Correct: the first week column is dated this Saturday, and the orange starting-cash cell in that column is empty. New Week clears it on purpose, because the next thing you do is fill it with the real number.
Do not click New Week again, not even to see whether it worked. Check the date instead.
A message appears about a worksheet missing data, or the first date did not change: part of the roll did not happen even though the success box appeared. Close Excel without saving, delete this week's copy, and go back to Step 1 with last week's file. Note what the message said in the box below.
A message says you can only exit the AR Collection module with the large blue button: you were on the wrong sheet and nothing was moved. Click the large blue button at the top of that sheet, then Current Rolling Quarter, then try New Week once.
Nothing happens at all when you click it: macros are off. Go back to Step 1.
5Type the real starting cash

This is the orange cell: the row (A) BEGINNING BALANCE CASH, first week column, at the top of the main sheet.

The number to type is your total from Step 3.

  1. Click the orange cell.
  2. Type the total, numbers only, no dollar sign. Press Enter.
Correct: the starting cash for week 2 onward changes by itself, all the way across. Only the first week's starting cash is ever typed.
Never type a starting cash into any later week. Those cells are formulas that carry each week's ending cash into the next. Typing over one breaks the chain for every week after it.
Why the real bank number and not the model's own guess: the model's ending cash for last week was a forecast. The bank balance is a fact. Starting every Monday from the fact is what stops small errors from piling up week after week.
6Put back what slipped

Anything on your slipped list from Step 3 rolled off the front with last week. If it is still going to happen, it has to go back in, in the week it will now really happen.

Your slipped list:

(nothing listed in Step 3)
  1. For money coming in, click Cash Receipts. For money going out, click Cash Expenses.
  2. If the item already has a row with its name, type the amount in the column for its new week.
  3. If it does not, use the next empty yellow row. Type a plain name in the name column, and the amount in the week column. Do not insert a row.
  4. Go back with Current Rolling Quarter.
Correct: the total inflow or total outflow for that week on the main sheet went up by exactly the amount you typed.
A slipped item turns out never to be happening (a customer who will not pay, a purchase you cancelled): leave it out. Note it below so the reason is on record.
7Update the money you expect from customers

This is the part of the model most likely to be optimistic. Money goes in the week you have good reason to expect it, not the week you would like it.

  1. In QuickBooks, left menu Reports, search Accounts receivable aging detail and open it. It lists every open invoice and how late it is.
  2. In the workbook, click Cash Receipts.
  3. A customer promised a date and an amount: give that payment its own row, named for the customer, with the amount in the week of the promised date.
  4. A general line for ordinary collections, if your workbook has one, covers the steady trickle of smaller payments. When you give a payment its own row, check that the general line is not also counting it. The same dollar must never be in two rows.
  5. Invoices more than 90 days late with no promise: leave them out of the next 13 weeks entirely. If one arrives, it is a pleasant surprise rather than a hole.
  6. Any other money coming in that is not from an invoice (a refund, a deposit you are owed) also goes here, in its week.

Example with made-up numbers: a customer owes 6,000 and promised it for the week ending the 17th. It gets its own row with 6,000 in that week. If your general collections line was built to include that customer, lower the general line so the 6,000 is not counted twice.

Why it matters: every other number in the model is fairly certain, because bills and rent arrive on schedule. Customer money is the one that moves. A model that assumes everyone pays on time will show you comfortable weeks that never come.
Leave the Receivables line alone. On the main sheet, Received on A/R comes from a separate invoice-import feature. If it reads zero in every week, your workbook keeps customer money in Cash Receipts instead. Keep it that way; turning on the import as well would count the same invoices twice.

Chasing late invoices is its own job: Clear Out Aged Receivables, Steps 2, 3 and 7.

8Update the money going out

Payments live in one of three places. Each payment belongs in exactly one of them.

ButtonWhat goes thereWhat to do this week
Recurring Expenses Anything that repeats on a schedule: payroll, rent, a lease, loan payments, insurance, utilities, subscriptions. Nothing, usually. The workbook places these in the right weeks by itself. Change a line only when an amount changed, or when something started or stopped.
Cash Expenses One-off payments and bills with a known date: a supplier bill, a tax payment, a repair, a quarterly bill. Add new bills in the week you will pay them. Remove any you paid early.
Investments Large planned payments for a project, for example a consulting or professional fee. Check each one is in the week it will actually clear, as one whole amount.

Recurring Expenses, when something changed

  1. Click Recurring Expenses.
  2. Each line has a name, a frequency, a day, and an amount. Frequency is a single letter: w weekly, b every two weeks, m monthly. For a monthly line, the day is the day of the month it comes out.
  3. To change an amount, type the new amount over the old one.
  4. To add a new repeating payment, use the next empty yellow line in the lower section.
  5. To stop one, clear its name, frequency, day and amount. Leave the first column alone; it is a formula and turns itself off.
Match the letter to how the amount was worked out. A monthly figure typed with b is counted about 26 times a year instead of 12, so the model shows more than twice the real cost.

Cash Expenses, for this week's bills

  1. In QuickBooks, Reports → search Unpaid bills to see what is waiting. Your mail and email may hold bills not entered yet.
  2. In the workbook, click Cash Expenses.
  3. Each bill goes in the week you will pay it, which is not always the week it is due. Type the whole amount in that one week.
  4. New payee: next empty yellow row, plain name, amount in the week. Do not insert rows.
Big one-off payments go in whole. If a large check will clear in one piece, the model shows it in one piece, in one week. Splitting it into smaller amounts across several weeks hides the week that actually hurts.
Never count a payment twice. Something set up on Recurring Expenses must not also be typed on Cash Expenses. And if you pay for things on a credit card, the model either shows the things you bought or shows the card payment, never both. Keep whichever way your workbook already does it.
Correct: on the main sheet, TOTAL OUTFLOW for each week reads like a week you recognize, and the week of any big payment stands out.
9Read the ending cash for all 13 weeks

Click Current Rolling Quarter and find the row (E.1) ENDING CASH AVAILABLE. That row is the whole point of the workbook: the cash left at the end of each of the next 13 Saturdays.

  1. Run your eye along the row and find the lowest number. Type it here with its week.
  2. Type the last number in the row, week 13, as well.
What you seeWhat it means
Every week comfortably above zero The plan, as entered, pays everything on time. Keep the dates honest and check again next Monday.
A week that is low but positive You get through it, but one late customer payment would tip it over. Treat it as a warning and start on Step 10 early.
A week below zero If nothing changes, the bank account runs dry that week: payments bounce, fees land, and automatic drafts fail. It has not happened yet. You can see it coming, and that is the whole value of the model. Go to Step 10.
Week 13 lower than this week's starting cash Over the quarter, more is going out than coming in. Even if no single week goes negative, the direction matters more than any one week.
Why the lowest week and not the last one: a quarter can end fine and still have one week in the middle where the account is empty. That single week is the one that bounces a payment.
10If a week goes below zero

Work through these in order. Each time you change something, look at the ending cash row again; stop when the lowest week is back above zero with some room to spare.

  1. Make sure it is real. Look at that week's payments. Is anything in twice, in the wrong week, or already paid? Fixing a mistake is the cheapest way to close a gap.
  2. Bring money in sooner. Invoice any finished work today (Create and Send an Invoice). Call the customers with the largest late invoices and ask for a date. Only a promised date moves money into an earlier week in the model.
  3. Move a payment that can genuinely wait, but only once the payee has agreed to the new date. Move the whole amount to the new week in Cash Expenses. A payment you simply stop making, without agreement, turns into late fees or a stopped service.
  4. Leave the fixed obligations where they are. Payroll, tax deposits, and loan payments the lender drafts automatically come out on schedule whatever the model says. Moving them in the workbook changes only the workbook.
  5. Never split a big payment to hide a bad week. If a large payment cannot be made in one piece that week, move the whole payment to a week that can carry it, once that date is agreed.
Nothing on the list closes the gap: note the week, the amount it is short, and what you tried, in the box below. You now know about the problem weeks before the bank does, which is exactly what the model is for.
11Save, close, and leave last week's file alone
  1. Click Current Rolling Quarter so the workbook opens on the main sheet next time.
  2. Press Ctrl + S to save. The name stays the one you gave it in Step 2.
  3. Close Excel.
  4. Do not open last week's file to change anything. It is the record of what you expected then, and your way back if this week's copy ever goes wrong.
Done well: the folder has one new file with this Saturday's date, its first week starts from the real bank balance, nothing that slipped has been lost, and you know which week is the tightest and by how much.
