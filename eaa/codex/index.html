What Happens Here

A working aviation chapter, not a mailing list.

The site should make the chapter easy to discover, easy to trust, and easy to visit. These are the public doors most people need first.

Builder Community

Homebuilt aircraft projects, hangar problem-solving, hands-on experience, and the kind of practical knowledge that only moves by being shared.

Young Eagles

A clear landing place for parents and students to understand youth flight opportunities, expectations, signups, and event-day basics.

Flight Advisors

Support for pilots preparing for first flights, unfamiliar aircraft, proficiency decisions, and safer transitions into experimental aviation.

Technical Counselors

Experienced eyes on aircraft projects before a small oversight becomes a big problem. Practical guidance, not bureaucracy.

IMC Club

Scenario-based conversations for instrument pilots and students who want sharper judgment when weather and workload get real.

Member Projects

A public archive of aircraft, restorations, shop updates, photos, and the stories that make a local chapter worth joining.

Chapter Mission

Make aviation local, useful, and reachable.

Facebook handles fast updates. The EAA chapter page handles the official directory. This site is the front desk: what the chapter is, why it matters, and what to do next.

1Show up clearly.
Meeting details, airport context, and current chapter activity should be visible in seconds.
2Serve newcomers.
Parents, students, pilots, and builders each need a different first answer.
3Keep it current.
Events, newsletters, photos, and projects need simple update paths so the site stays alive.
